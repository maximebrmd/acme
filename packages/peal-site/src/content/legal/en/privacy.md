## Personal data we process

These categories follow the service as it is designed. [Confirm each one against the released service.]

- **Account data**: your name, email address and sign-in details. [List the sign-in methods offered.]
- **Organisation data**: the organisations you belong to, your role in each, and the invitations you send or receive.
- **Connected accounts**: the social media accounts you connect, their identifiers on each platform, and the access tokens the platforms issue to Peal.
- **Content**: the posts, media and schedules you create, the publication history of each post, and how each post was created and scheduled (in the app, with an API key or through an application you connected, and by which member); per organisation and month, how many posts went out on X, and how many held a link.
- **Performance data**: the metrics the platforms report for your posts, and for the accounts you connected: their followers day by day, and their views where the platform counts them.
- **Advertising data**, when an organisation connects ad accounts: the sign-in a member makes to the ad network (their identifier there, the permissions they gave, when the sign-in ends, and its access token, stored encrypted); the ad accounts connected, as the network describes them (identifier, name, currency, time zone, status, spend cap and amount spent), the monthly ceiling an owner set, who connected each and who allowed it to spend; their campaigns, ad sets and ads as read from the network, and each ad's daily numbers (counts and money, never who saw an ad); the boosts asked for (the post, the budget, the dates, the audience as countries and an age range, who benefits from the ad and who pays for it when the audience is in the European Union, and who asked); and the audit trail of every decision about spending, with who made it and the amounts.
- **Webhooks**: the addresses an organisation has Peal call when something happens in it, with the values of the headers it chose for them (such as a token its receiving server asks for, stored encrypted and never shown again), and the log of each delivery (the event's identifiers, states and times, never what a post says, and how the receiving server answered).
- **Usage counts**: per organisation and day, how many posts went out on each platform and how many requests it made to the writing assistant, and, as the daily count found them shortly after the day ended, how many channels and members it had and how much media it stored. Counts only, never what a post says or which member did what.
- **Short links and their click counts**: when a post's author asks for its links to be shortened, each short link's code, the address it leads to (the organisation's own content), the post, channel and part it is in, and how many times it was followed each day. Counts only: nothing about the people who follow a link, neither their IP address, their browser, the page they came from nor their country.
- **Billing data**: your plan, subscription status and invoices; each order placed before paying, with the consent given and the version of its words; the trial each account and organisation started; each withdrawal and what it refunded; each request to cancel a contract or to withdraw from it, with the date and time we received it and what became of it, and, when it came through the form that needs no account, what you wrote there (your name, the email address for the acknowledgement, the contract, the day wanted). Payments are handled by [Payment provider]. [State which payment details, if any, we receive.]
- **Technical data**: IP addresses, browser details and logs produced when you use the service, and, for each connection that creates, changes or deletes content, its time, IP address and protocol.
- **Communications**: the messages you send us and the emails we send you.
- **Comments on your posts, and people who are not users**: when an organisation's post is live on a platform where Peal reads comments (Mastodon and Bluesky), the comments people leave under it in public: each one's text, when it was written, what it answers, and its author's public handle, display name and identifier on the platform, as the platform gives them to anyone. We read nothing else of these people: no picture, no profile, no list of followers, no private message. We keep whether the organisation answered a comment, and the replies its members send from Peal. We process these comments for the organisation, which decides what it answers, and use them for nothing else: we build no profile of their authors, show them no advertising, take no automated decision about them and train no model on what they wrote. A comment deleted on its platform loses its text and its author in Peal the next time we read the post. If you commented under a post and want to exercise your rights, write to the organisation that published it or to us (see Contact). We take no email address from a reviewer, and send them nothing. Members' comments and decisions are kept with their account. We process a reviewer's data for the organisation that sent the link. ## Data from connected platforms

When you connect a social media account, the platform shares data with Peal within the permissions you grant. Peal uses that data to publish the posts you schedule and to show how they perform. On Mastodon and Bluesky, Peal also reads the public replies to the posts it published for you, to show them to your organisation and let its members answer. [Describe any other use, and confirm that each use complies with the developer terms of every platform Peal connects to.]

When a member signs in to an ad network to connect ad accounts, the network shares, within the permissions that member grants, the ad accounts they can reach, then the campaigns, ads and daily numbers of the accounts the organisation connects. Peal uses them to show what the ads did, and to run the boosts members ask for. The member who signed in can ask the network to delete what Peal holds about them: Peal then removes their sign-ins and the ad accounts these connect.

[Confirm once built: you can disconnect an account in Peal.] You can also revoke Peal's access from the platform's own settings.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR, you have the right to:

- access the personal data held about you (Article 15);
- have it corrected (Article 16);
- have it erased (Article 17): [Data deletion](/data-deletion/) explains how;
- restrict its processing (Article 18);
- receive it in a structured, machine-readable format (Article 20);
- object to processing based on legitimate interests (Article 21);
- withdraw your consent at any time where processing relies on it, without affecting the processing that came before (Article 7(3)).

Closing your account erases your data at once, except the identification data above, which French law requires us to keep (Article 17(3)(b)); your copy of your data lists it, and you can still ask for it by writing to privacy@peal.social. You can download a copy of your data at any time from your account settings, or with the command line; we email you each time a copy is downloaded, so a copy someone else took does not go unnoticed. To exercise these rights, write to privacy@peal.social. The GDPR requires an answer within one month, which can be extended in some cases (Article 12(3)).
