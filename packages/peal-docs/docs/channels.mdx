A channel is a social account connected to a workspace: a Mastodon, Bluesky, LinkedIn, X, Threads, Instagram or TikTok account, a Facebook Page, or a YouTube channel, today. Posts go to channels, so connect at least one before you schedule anything.

Open a workspace from **Workspaces**, then **Channels**. Every member sees the workspace's channels; only owners and admins connect, reconnect and disconnect them.

<Callout type="note" title="Nine platforms so far">
  Peal connects Mastodon, Bluesky, LinkedIn, X, Threads, Instagram and TikTok
  accounts, Facebook Pages and YouTube channels. The other platform it knows,
  Pinterest, cannot be connected yet. **Channels** offers only the platforms the
  Peal server is set up for.
</Callout>

## Connect an account [#connect]

You sign in on the platform, agree to what Peal asks, and the platform sends you back to Peal, which finishes the connection. Finish it in the same browser tab you started it in, within 15 minutes.

Connecting an account the workspace already has reconnects its channel rather than adding a second one. Several workspaces may connect the same account.

### Mastodon [#mastodon]

Under **Mastodon**, give the account's server, such as `mastodon.social`, or the account's own address, such as `@you@mastodon.social`, then choose **Connect Mastodon**.

Peal works with any Mastodon server on the public internet, reached over HTTPS. The first time someone connects an account on a server, Peal registers itself there as an application. It asks to read the account's profile, to publish posts and media, and to read the account's posts back, for their counts in [analytics](/analytics). An account connected before Peal asked to read its posts keeps posting as it did; reconnect it to grant that too.

### Bluesky [#bluesky]

Under **Bluesky**, give your handle, such as `you.bsky.social`, to go straight to your server's sign-in page, or leave it empty to pick the account on Bluesky. Then choose **Connect Bluesky**.

The Peal server must be set up for Bluesky: on a self-hosted instance, see [Bluesky](/self-hosting#bluesky). Until then, **Channels** does not offer it.

### LinkedIn [#linkedin]

Under **LinkedIn**, choose **Connect LinkedIn**, sign in on LinkedIn and allow Peal. Peal connects your own LinkedIn profile, and posts as you; company pages cannot be connected yet. It asks LinkedIn for your name and picture, and to publish posts.

LinkedIn lets Peal post for 60 days, then asks you to sign in again: the channel shows the date, and its workspace's owners and admins are told a week before. Reconnect before then: when you are signed in on LinkedIn, it does not ask again.

The Peal server must be set up for LinkedIn: on a self-hosted instance, see [LinkedIn](/self-hosting#linkedin). Until then, **Channels** does not offer it.

### X [#x]

Under **X**, choose **Connect X**, sign in on X and allow Peal. It asks to read and write posts, to upload media, and to stay connected, so it can renew its access without you.

The Peal server must be set up for X: on a self-hosted instance, see [X](/self-hosting#x). Until then, **Channels** does not offer it.

X counts a post's length its own way: a link counts 23 characters whatever its length, written with `https://` or without, as `peal.social` (a domain X knows, so not `localhost`), and an emoji, or a character outside the Latin, Greek and Cyrillic scripts, counts 2. Peal counts the same, so a post it accepts fits on X (see [Length on each channel](/scheduling#write)).

### TikTok [#tiktok]

Under **TikTok**, choose **Connect TikTok**, sign in on TikTok and allow Peal. It asks for your profile and username, to post videos and photos on your account, and to read your videos, for their counts in [analytics](/analytics). You may turn the last one off on TikTok's page: the channel then posts without analytics.

The Peal server must be set up for TikTok: on a self-hosted instance, see [TikTok](/self-hosting#tiktok). Until then, **Channels** does not offer it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Until TikTok audits the server's app">
  TikTok takes only private posts from an app it has not audited: the account
  must be private when the post goes out, and the post visible to you only
  (**Only me**). Any other post fails with "the platform takes only private
  posts from this server's app". Ask the server's operator whether TikTok
  audited it.
</Callout>

### Facebook Pages [#facebook]

Under **Facebook Pages**, choose **Connect Facebook Pages**, sign in with Facebook, and allow Peal to list your Pages and post on them. Back in Peal, tick the Pages the workspace posts to, then **Connect**: each becomes a channel. A sign-in that reaches one Page connects it straight away. Only Pages you may create content on are listed, and those the workspace already has are marked: choosing one reconnects it. Choose within 15 minutes. When connecting fails before any Page is connected, Peal says why, and you can choose again, or **Sign in with Facebook again** for a new list; once the choice has expired or been made, only signing in again brings the Pages back.

### Instagram [#instagram]

Peal posts to Instagram professional accounts, business or creator. It reaches them in one of two ways, which **Instagram** offers as two buttons when the server has both:

- **Connect with Instagram** (Instagram Login), the default: sign in to the professional account itself on Instagram, and allow Peal to read its profile, publish, and read its insights. The account needs no Facebook Page. Its access lasts 60 days, and Peal renews it on its own a week before it ends. You may decline insights: the account then posts, without counts in **Analytics**.
- **Connect through a Facebook Page** (Facebook Login): sign in with Facebook, as for Pages, then tick the Instagram accounts linked to your Pages. An account posts with its Page's access, which does not expire, so it stays connected as long as you manage the Page. Use it for an account run from a Page, or for what only Meta's Facebook Login API gives, such as tagging products, collaborators and hashtag search, which Peal does not use yet.

Both publish images, reels and carousels the same way, and read the same counts. The channels list says which way each Instagram channel came through, and reconnecting it signs in that way again. Connecting through Instagram Login an account the workspace has through a Page moves its channel there, with its posts and schedule: the workspace keeps one channel per account.

### Threads [#threads]

Under **Threads**, choose **Connect Threads**, sign in with your Threads account, and allow Peal to read your profile and publish.

### YouTube [#youtube]

Under **YouTube**, choose **Connect YouTube**, sign in with Google, and pick the channel: a Google account that manages several channels, its brand accounts' included, lists them on Google's account chooser, and each connection is one channel. Allow Peal to upload videos and to see your YouTube account: it reads the channel's name and picture, its videos' processing and their counts, and changes nothing else. Untick either, and the connection is refused. A Google account without a channel connects nothing: create the channel on YouTube first.

The Peal server must be set up for YouTube: on a self-hosted instance, see [YouTube](/self-hosting#youtube). Until then, **Channels** does not offer it.

## Statuses [#statuses]

Each channel shows one of these:

| Status           | What it means                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Connected        | Peal can post to the account.                                                                                                                                       |
| Reconnect needed | The platform no longer accepts Peal's access. Nothing goes out on the channel until you reconnect it.                                                               |
| Turned off       | Nothing goes out on the channel, and reconnecting leaves it off. To post to the account again, [disconnect](#disconnect) the channel and connect the account again. |

A channel whose access cannot be renewed also shows the date by which it must be reconnected, and its workspace's owners and admins are told ahead of that date.

## Keep channels connected [#keep-connected]

Peal renews each account's access on its own before it runs out: it refreshes a Bluesky session a month before it would lapse, X access, which lasts two hours, whenever it posts, and at least once a month for an account that does not post, so X never drops it, Threads access and the access of an Instagram account connected with Instagram, which last 60 days, a week before, TikTok access, which lasts a day or less, half an hour before it ends, and YouTube access, which lasts an hour, every hour. TikTok lets Peal renew it for 180 days after you connected the account: then the channel asks to be reconnected. Google ends YouTube access when the owner removes Peal from their Google account, or six months after Peal last used it; on a self-hosted server whose Google project is in testing, seven days after the channel was connected. Mastodon access, and the Page access Facebook Pages and Instagram accounts connected through a Page post with, do not expire until someone revokes them; Facebook also withdraws a Page's access when the person who connected it changes their password or loses their role on the Page. LinkedIn access cannot be renewed: reconnect the channel every 60 days, as above. When someone removes Peal from their Facebook, Instagram or Threads account, Meta tells Peal, which marks the channels that person connected to be reconnected, in every workspace, and tells the members who manage them, before a post fails on them.

When a platform refuses Peal's access while Peal posts, Peal renews the access where the platform allows it, and tries once more. When the platform refuses again, or the access cannot be renewed, for example because the account's owner revoked it, the channel turns to **Reconnect needed**, and the workspace's owners and admins are told once, under the bell and by email unless they turned that email off (see [Notifications](/notifications)). When a platform does not answer, Peal tries again later and leaves the channel as it is.

## What Peal posts [#posting]

At a post's time, Peal posts it as the channel's account, with its text on that channel and its images and videos, each with its alt text when it has one. [Images and videos](/media#platforms) lists what each platform accepts.

- **Mastodon**: Peal posts on the account's server, with the account's default visibility. The server turns mentions, hashtags and links into links itself, and may process a video before the post can show it.
- **Bluesky**: Peal posts on the server that holds the account. Links and hashtags become links; mentions stay plain text for now. Videos go through Bluesky's video service, which processes them before the post goes out.
- **LinkedIn**: Peal posts publicly, as the member. Hashtags become LinkedIn hashtags; mentions stay plain text. LinkedIn processes a video before the post goes out.
- **X**: Peal posts as the account. X turns mentions, hashtags and links into links itself, a domain without `https://` such as `peal.social` too, and processes a video before the post goes out. When X refuses the server's app because its credits ran out, the post is not at fault: Peal waits for over two hours, as for an outage, then the post fails with its reason (see [Notifications](/notifications)).
- **Threads** and **Instagram**: Meta fetches each image and video from the media store, processes it, then Peal publishes the post: several files make a carousel. An Instagram video goes out as a reel. Peal checks the account's daily publishing limit before it starts.
- **Facebook Pages**: Peal posts the text on the Page, a photo with it, several photos in one post, or a video, which Facebook fetches from the media store.
- **TikTok**: Peal posts one video, with the post's text as its caption, or up to 35 photos, with the text as their description, as you chose in the post's [TikTok options](/scheduling#options). Just before, it reads the account's settings on TikTok again, and the post fails if they no longer allow it. A video goes up in parts from the media store, unless TikTok can fetch it itself (see [TikTok](/self-hosting#tiktok)); photos TikTok always fetches. TikTok then processes the post, which Peal follows until it is published: a post others see shows a link to it once TikTok's moderation passed it, one only you see has none. TikTok allows about 15 posts a day to each account, shared by every app.
- **YouTube**: Peal uploads the post's video to the channel, with the post's text as its description and the title, visibility, made-for-kids answer, tags and category of its [platform options](/scheduling#options), then waits while YouTube processes it. A video YouTube fails to process or rejects, as a duplicate or for its length, fails with YouTube's reason. When YouTube keeps a video private although you chose public or unlisted, the post's page says so: change its visibility in YouTube Studio. A Peal server uploads a limited number of videos a day, across all its channels: past that, a video waits until midnight Pacific Time, when YouTube's quotas reset, and the post's page shows the wait.

## Reconnect [#reconnect]

Choose **Reconnect** next to the channel, then sign in on the platform with the same account. Signing in with another account is refused: to post to it, connect it as a new channel.

## Disconnect [#disconnect]

Choose **Disconnect** next to the channel and confirm. Peal deletes the tokens it holds for the account and posts nothing more to it. You can connect it again later.

Disconnecting does not tell the platform, except TikTok and YouTube: unless another workspace still connects the same account, Peal revokes its access there, and leaves the account's connected apps. To remove Peal's access on the other platforms, revoke it in the account's settings there.

## When connecting fails [#errors]

| Message                                                          | What to do                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| This server is not set up to connect accounts of this platform   | Ask the instance's administrator: the server lacks a setting the platform needs.                                                                                        |
| This is not the address of a server                              | Check the Mastodon server's address, for example `mastodon.social`.                                                                                                     |
| Peal only connects to servers on the public internet, over HTTPS | The server is private, or does not answer over HTTPS.                                                                                                                   |
| No account was found for this handle                             | Check the Bluesky handle.                                                                                                                                               |
| This connection expired or was started elsewhere                 | Start again from the workspace's channels, in this tab.                                                                                                                 |
| The connection was cancelled on the platform                     | You, or the platform, refused. Start again if you meant to connect.                                                                                                     |
| You signed in to another account than this channel's             | Reconnect with the channel's own account.                                                                                                                               |
| The sign-in reached no account to connect                        | Manage a Facebook Page, or link your Instagram professional account to one, or connect it with Instagram instead; on YouTube, pick a Google account that has a channel. |
| The platform did not answer                                      | Try again in a moment.                                                                                                                                                  |
