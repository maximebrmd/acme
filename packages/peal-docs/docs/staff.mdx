The staff console is for the people who run a Peal server: it finds an account or a workspace, tells how the server is doing, and lets staff act on accounts. It lives at `/admin` on your Peal address (`/fr/admin` in French), and its API under `/api/admin`.

## Make an account staff [#make-staff]

Staff are accounts with Better Auth's admin role. No page grants it: set it in the database, then sign in again, as a session keeps the role it started with.

```sql
UPDATE auth."user" SET role = 'admin' WHERE email = 'you@example.com';
```

To take the role back, set `role` to `NULL`, then have other staff **Sign out everywhere** the account. The sessions it opened to act as someone else end on their next request, whatever it is, as the server checks on each one that their staff member is still staff and not banned, and the audit log records each end. The console refuses anyone but staff in their own session, signed in or not, answering 404 as if it were not there: an API key, an application acting through OAuth, or staff while they act as someone else are refused too. A banned staff account is refused at once.

Peal's own servers also put `/admin`, `/en/admin`, `/fr/admin` and `/api/admin` behind Cloudflare Access. Behind your own edge, you may restrict them to your team's addresses the same way.

## What the console shows [#screens]

| Page              | What it shows                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| System            | Whether the database answers, the release the server runs (`OTEL_SERVICE_VERSION`), the posts due within the hour, those overdue, and the channels to reconnect in every workspace |
| Accounts          | Accounts by email, name or id, newest first, 25 a page                                                                                                                             |
| An account        | Its name and email, whether the address is confirmed, its language, when it was created and last signed in, its ban, its workspaces and roles, and how many sessions it has open   |
| Workspaces        | Workspaces by name, slug or id, with their members                                                                                                                                 |
| A workspace       | Its members and roles, its plan and subscription, its channels and whether each works, how many posts it has in each status, and the latest posts that failed or went unconfirmed  |
| Contract requests | The requests to cancel a contract or withdraw from one that wait for staff, oldest first                                                                                           |
| Audit log         | What staff did, newest first                                                                                                                                                       |

Staff see who an account is, never what it wrote: no post's text, no image or video, no token. A failed post shows as its ids, its platform, why it failed and the platform's code. To see what a member sees, staff act as them (see below).

"Last signed in" is when the newest session the account opened itself started, while one is left; sessions staff open to act as it do not count.

## What staff do [#actions]

Each action asks why, and keeps the reason in the audit log with the staff member's name. Write what was asked, not more about the person than needed.

- **Ban**, for a day, a week, 30 days or until lifted: the account is signed out everywhere, sessions it opened as staff to act as someone else included, and can neither sign in nor use its API keys meanwhile. **Lift the ban** undoes it.
- **Sign out everywhere** ends every session of the account, on every device, those staff opened to act as it and those it opened as staff to act as someone else included. Its API keys keep working.
- **Send the confirmation again** emails a new link to confirm the address, in the account's language, while it is not confirmed.
- **Act as this account** signs the staff member's browser in as the account for an hour. A banner at the top of every page says whose account it is, with **Stop acting as them**, which brings back the staff member's own session.

While acting as an account, staff see its posts and media, and can write and edit drafts in its name; they cannot schedule or publish, connect channels, manage API keys or billing, change its workspaces or the account, or let an application act for it. They can read its notifications, not mark them read. They can ask the writing assistant in its workspaces, which counts toward those workspaces' daily requests. What they make carries the account's name, as its author, and the audit log keeps who made it (see below).

Staff never ban, sign out or act as themselves, and never act as other staff. No one deletes an account but its owner, from [their account settings](/settings#delete-account).

Better Auth's own admin endpoints under `/api/auth/admin/` are off, so nothing staff do bypasses the audit log; only `POST /api/auth/admin/stop-impersonating` stays, which the banner calls.

## Contract requests [#contract-requests]

On a server that bills, anyone cancels a contract or withdraws from one without signing in ([Plans and billing](/billing#cancelling)). A request that gives its contract number, or whose sender follows the link emailed to an address that manages a contract, is applied without anyone's help. **Contract requests** lists the others: those no contract was found for, and those whose sender has not followed their link yet. Each shows what its sender wrote, and nothing else: their name, the address their acknowledgement went to, the contract as they named it, the day wanted, and the account that address signs in to, if any.

- **Apply to this workspace** ties the request to a workspace's contract, by the workspace's id: do it only once you know the sender holds that contract, as by writing to the address the account has on file. It takes effect as of the date the request was received, whenever you apply it: a renewal charged since is refunded, and a withdrawal's 14 days and refund count from that date. The sender is emailed what became of it.
- **Close without applying**, for a request sent twice or by someone who holds no contract. The sender is not written to: write to them yourself when they need an answer.

Both ask why, and are in the audit log with the request's id and the workspace's, never the name or the address the request holds. Requests are kept 5 years, as the contract's orders are.

## The audit log [#audit-log]

Each action is recorded before it is carried out, so nothing staff do goes unrecorded: who did it, what, to which account, why, and when, with a ban's end. An action that then failed still shows what staff asked for. The log keeps ids, never what anyone wrote. It keeps each entry 12 months, and a ban, or the lifting of one, 5 years (the limitation period for a claim about it, art. 2224 of the French Code civil), or 12 months past the end of a ban that lasted longer; a ban until lifted is kept as long as it holds, and ends when it is lifted or the account is closed: a daily job (`staff.audit-retention`, at 03:41 UTC) deletes older entries.

While staff act as an account, the server records every request that changes something, before it runs, whichever way it comes: Peal's API (`/api/…`) and the sign-in service's routes (`/api/auth/…`) alike. The handler of the request has no say in it, so a change cannot skip its record, and a change the server then refuses is recorded too. Each entry says:

| Field     | What it holds                                                                                                                   |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Staff     | The staff member who acted                                                                                                      |
| Account   | The account they acted as                                                                                                       |
| Action    | Changed something as the account                                                                                                |
| Operation | The API's endpoint, as `group.endpoint` (`posts.create`, `posts.schedule`), or the sign-in route, as `auth:/path`               |
| Workspace | The workspace it was in                                                                                                         |
| Ids       | The ids the request named, by name (`postId`, `channelId`, `organizationId`); never the text, media or anything else it carried |
| When      | When it was asked for                                                                                                           |

Reads are not recorded. MCP clients, the command line and API keys never act for staff: their tokens carry no impersonation, and staff acting as an account may not let an application act for it or create a key. A request to delete one of its workspaces or close the account is refused, so no subscription is cancelled, and recorded like any other change. These entries are kept 12 months, like the rest of the log.

The log also records how each impersonation ended: **Stopped acting as the account** when staff stop it from the banner or sign out, and **Acting as the account ended, by the server** when the server ends it, as its staff member was banned, signed out everywhere or is no longer staff, or the account was banned or signed out everywhere. One that is never stopped ends an hour after it began, as its start in the log shows.

When an account is closed, the log forgets it as the account acted on: entries about it lose its id, the reason given, the workspace and the ids a change named, and its id leaves every other entry that names it. A ban, or the lifting of one, keeps its reason, with a keyed pseudonym of the account's email address instead of its id, for its 5 years: whoever runs the server may have to show a court what it did and why, and can find the entry again only from that address. Entries it made as staff keep their author, as a legitimate interest: whoever runs the server answers for what staff did to other people's accounts, after staff leave too. What was done, and when, stays. The account's data download lists what staff did to it, with the reason given and the changes staff made as it (what, where and on which ids), but not who did it.

## Legal requests [#legal-requests]

A judicial authority may ask, in writing, for the data that identifies who contributed content (loi n° 2004-575, Article 6, V, B). A server that keeps it ([Legal retention](/self-hosting#legal-retention)) answers from **Legal requests** in the console (`/admin/legal`), which staff see only with the legal role too: give it to the few people who answer authorities, and no one else, with `UPDATE auth."user" SET role = 'admin,legal' WHERE email = 'you@example.com';`, then sign in again. Other staff get the not-found page there, and 404 from its API, like anyone else.

Fill in the request from the authority's document:

- what it names: an email address (the closed accounts that had it), an account's id (closed or not), or a workspace's id;
- the authority, the request's reference, and its legal basis, the text that gives the authority the power to ask;
- what it asks for, and nothing more: who held the account, how it signed in, its payment references, the connections that created, changed or deleted content, and the period of those connections when the request names one.

**Record the request and show the answer** records it first, then shows only what the request covers: the closed accounts that match, and the connections of those accounts, of the account named, or in the workspace named. A category the request did not ask for reads **Not covered by this request**, and the server sends none of it. How an account signed in and paid is kept {{keep-legal-account-en}} after it closed, who held it {{keep-legal-identity-en}}, and each connection {{keep-legal-connections-en}}.

This is the only way to read what a closed account left. Each answer is recorded, before it is given, with the staff member, the authority, its reference, its legal basis, what was asked about and for, and how many records it returned, never the data handed over, and the record is kept {{keep-legal-disclosures-en}}. The page says so before anything is entered: tell the staff you give the role to that their answers are recorded and reviewed, as employment law may require of you. **Answers given** lists the records, newest first, and each shows in the audit log too, as **Answered a legal request** with the authority and its reference, so the rest of staff see it. Enter only a request an authority made in writing, and keep the request itself with your legal records. A live account's details are on its page in the console; only the connections need a legal request.

The same is available to scripts, as staff in their own session: `POST /api/admin/legal-requests` answers a request, and `GET /api/admin/legal-requests` lists the answers given.
