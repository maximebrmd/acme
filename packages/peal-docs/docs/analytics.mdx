Once a post goes live, Peal reads its counts back from the platform for a month: likes, reposts and replies, and the counts only some platforms tell. It also reads each connected account once a day, from the day you connect it: its followers, and its views where the platform counts them. Open a workspace from **Workspaces**, then **Analytics**, to see them. Every member may, whatever their role.

## What Peal counts on a post [#counts]

| Platform       | Likes      | Reposts        | Replies  | Quotes   | Impressions | Clicks | Reach              | Saves     |
| -------------- | ---------- | -------------- | -------- | -------- | ----------- | ------ | ------------------ | --------- |
| Mastodon       | Favourites | Boosts         | Replies  | From 4.5 | –           | –      | –                  | –         |
| Bluesky        | Likes      | Reposts        | Replies  | Quotes   | –           | –      | –                  | –         |
| Threads        | Likes      | Reposts        | Replies  | Quotes   | Views       | –      | –                  | –         |
| Instagram      | Likes      | Shares         | Comments | –        | Views       | –      | –                  | –         |
| Facebook Pages | Reactions  | Shares         | Comments | –        | –           | –      | –                  | –         |
| LinkedIn pages | Likes      | Shares         | Comments | –        | Impressions | Clicks | Unique impressions | –         |
| TikTok         | Likes      | Shares         | Comments | –        | Views       | –      | –                  | –         |
| YouTube        | Likes      | None, always 0 | Comments | –        | Views       | –      | –                  | –         |
| Pinterest      | Reactions  | Saves          | Comments | –        | Impressions | –      | –                  | –         |
| X              | Likes      | Reposts        | Replies  | Quotes   | Impressions | –      | –                  | Bookmarks |

A dash is a count the platform does not give, or one Peal may not read there: it is shown as missing, never as zero. **Engagement** is likes, reposts, replies and quotes together. On Facebook Pages, comments count the replies to comments too.

Three platforms need more than connecting the account:

- **Threads**: Meta gives a post's insights to apps it granted `threads_manage_insights`. Until the server's app has it and the account allowed it, Threads posts are passed over.
- **Facebook Pages**: reactions, comments and shares are read with what Peal asks today. A post's views and clicks are a Page's insights, which need `read_insights`, a permission Meta grants after a review: Peal reads them for a Page connected with it, and shows none otherwise.
- **X**: X bills the app for each post a read returns. Peal reads nothing there unless the server's operator turned it on ([`X_ANALYTICS`](/environment-variables)), and each workspace then has a number of reads a day; past it, counts wait for the next day.

Peal reads no counts on LinkedIn profiles, which LinkedIn opens to few apps, nor on Google Business Profile, where Google counts views and clicks for a location as a whole, not for each post, since it withdrew its post insights in 2023. It finds so at a post's first turn, stops there, and leaves the post out of **Analytics**, rather than show it with no counts. A channel's [capabilities](/api-overview#capabilities) list the counts it has. Instagram and Facebook [stories](/scheduling#formats) disappear after 24 hours: Peal reads no counts of them, and they are not in **Analytics**.

## What Peal reads of an account [#accounts]

| Platform       | Followers                                  | Following | Posts            | Views by day | Reach by day       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------- | ---------------- | ------------ | ------------------ |
| Mastodon       | Yes                                        | Yes       | Yes              | –            | –                  |
| Bluesky        | Yes                                        | Yes       | Yes              | –            | –                  |
| Threads        | With `threads_manage_insights`             | –         | –                | –            | –                  |
| Instagram      | Yes                                        | Yes       | Yes              | –            | –                  |
| Facebook Pages | Yes                                        | –         | –                | –            | –                  |
| LinkedIn pages | Yes                                        | –         | –                | Impressions  | Unique impressions |
| TikTok         | With `user.info.stats`                     | Likewise  | Videos, likewise | –            | –                  |
| YouTube        | Subscribers, unless the channel hides them | –         | Public videos    | –            | –                  |
| Pinterest      | Yes                                        | Yes       | Pins             | Impressions  | –                  |
| X              | Where the operator turned X reads on       | Likewise  | Likewise         | –            | –                  |

Platforms rarely give the history of an account, so Peal keeps its own: one reading a day from the day the channel is connected, on the run of the hour it was first read. Followers before that day are not known, and a range that starts earlier counts what an account gained from its first reading. YouTube rounds subscribers down to three significant figures. A day's views on LinkedIn and Pinterest are read for the last three finished days, as both revise a day after it ends; days are UTC days. Peal reads no account numbers on LinkedIn profiles nor on Google Business Profile.

For a post of several parts, a [thread](/scheduling#threads), the counts are those of its first part: the replies that make the thread are not read one by one. The first part's replies include the thread's own second part, which is a reply to it. A thread is followed once all its parts are live: one that stopped midway has no counts until **Post again**, or what you found on the platform, completes it, and its 30 days start then.

## When counts are read [#schedule]

Peal reads a post's counts every hour for 48 hours after it goes live, when they move most, then every day until the post is 30 days old, then stops. The first reading comes within the hour after the post goes live.

Readings take turns with the channel's publishing: Peal makes one call at a time per account, so a reading never races a post going out. When a platform asks Peal to slow down, or keeps few requests for the account, Peal stops reading that channel and goes on at the next hour. A post the platform no longer shows, deleted or not public, waits for its next turn; so does one the platform fails to show, and Peal goes on with the channel's other posts at the next hour; a channel that is turned off or needs reconnecting is passed over until it works again, and a disconnected channel's posts are no longer read.

- **Mastodon**: Peal reads each post on the account's server, with the account's access when the account allowed Peal to read its posts. Accounts connected before Peal asked for that are read without it, which shows public and unlisted posts; [reconnect](/channels#reconnect) one to read its followers-only posts too.
- **Bluesky**: Peal reads posts from Bluesky's public service, 25 at a time, without the account's session: a post's counts are public on Bluesky.
- **TikTok**: Peal reads the account's public posts, 20 at a time, with the account's access, when the account allowed Peal to read its videos: their likes, shares as reposts, comments as replies, and views. TikTok gives no id for a post only its owner sees, so those are never read.
- **YouTube**: Peal reads the channel's videos with its access, 50 at a time, so private and unlisted videos are read too. A count the channel hides, such as its likes, reads as 0.
- **Instagram**: Peal reads each post's insights, one post at a time, with the account's access, when the account allowed Peal to read them: through Instagram Login, or through a Facebook Page connected since Peal asks for insights. An account that did not is passed over until you [reconnect](/channels#reconnect) it and allow them.
- **Pinterest**: Peal reads each Pin's analytics, one Pin at a time, with the account's access: its impressions and saves over the last 90 days, which cover the month Peal follows it, and its reactions and comments. A save puts the Pin on someone's board, so Peal counts it as a repost. Pinterest also counts Pin clicks and outbound clicks, which Peal does not show yet. Pinterest's figures for a day may be estimates until it finishes counting.
- **Threads**: Peal reads each post's insights, one post at a time, for an account that granted them.
- **Facebook Pages**: Peal reads each post with the Page's access, three requests a post: its shares, its reactions and its comments.
- **X**: Peal looks posts up 100 at a time. X bills each post once a UTC day however often it is read, and Peal counts a post once a day against the workspace's reads.
- **LinkedIn**: Peal reads the posts of a company page with the page's share statistics, all of a page's posts due in one request: impressions and unique impressions, clicks, likes, comments as replies and shares as reposts, organic only, over the past twelve months. LinkedIn gives them to the page's administrators: a page connected by a content administrator is passed over. A member's profile has no counts Peal may read.

Accounts are read once a day by a second hourly run, half an hour from the posts': it takes turns with publishing the same way, reads an account whose platform put it off at the next hour, and passes over a channel that is off for a day.

## How long figures are kept [#retention]

Peal keeps a post's counts and an account's days {{keep-analytics-en}}, then deletes them. Where a platform's terms for developers allow less, its cap applies: LinkedIn pages {{keep-analytics-linkedin-en}}, Pinterest {{keep-analytics-pinterest-en}}. A disconnected channel is no longer read from that moment, and its account leaves the analytics page; its account's days and its posts' counts are deleted {{keep-analytics-disconnected-en}} later. Connect the same account to the workspace again before that and they continue under the new channel. To give Peal a permission a channel lacks, connect the account again without disconnecting it: the channel stays, with all its figures. Deleting the workspace deletes everything.

## The analytics page [#page]

Pick the last 7, 30 or 90 days. Days run in your browser's time zone, or in UTC when the browser cannot tell it, and the range takes the posts published in it:

- **Against the days before**: posts, engagement, impressions and reach, each beside the same number of days just before the range, with what changed. Where a platform's terms keep its figures for less than the range before goes back (Pinterest over 90 days, a LinkedIn page over a longer range), Peal shows no change rather than one measured against figures it had to delete; the API says so in `previousKept`.
- **Followers**: how many follow the workspace's accounts at the end of the range, what they gained over it and over the range before, a chart of each day, and each channel's. Choose **Table** for the same figures as a table, with each day's views and reach, which is also what screen readers read.
- **Export**: the posts of the range with their counts, or the accounts day by day, as CSV files.
- **Totals**: how many posts went out, counting each channel a post went out on, and their latest counts, in all and by channel.
- **Engagement over time**: what those posts gained each day, as a chart, with its table.
- **Top posts**: the five posts with the most engagement, each with a link to it in Peal and on the platform.

## A post's counts [#post]

A post's page shows how it did on each channel it went out on: its latest counts, when they were read, and a chart of each reading, with the same figures as a table. A post that went out less than an hour ago says when its counts come, and one that went out on a platform Peal reads no counts on says they are not available there.

## Clicks on short links [#short-links]

A post that asked for [short links](/scheduling#short-links) counts how many times each was followed, by UTC day, per link, post and channel. A visit counts when a browser asks for the link: a request from an automated reader (a crawler, the link preview a platform or a messaging app makes), a prefetch, a `HEAD` request and a request without a `User-Agent` are redirected but not counted. Readers are told apart by the names they give themselves, which change, so the figure is approximate. Nothing about the visitor is kept: not their address, their browser, the page they came from or their country, only a count. Clicks are kept 400 days, by day, then deleted; the links go with their workspace.

The post's page lists its links and their clicks over the last 30 days to members who read analytics. Through the API, `GET /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/short-links` lists the workspace's links by cursor, newest first, those of a `postId` or a `channelId`, with their clicks from `from` to `to` (UTC days, 30 by default, 92 at most), and `GET …/short-links/{linkId}` answers one link with its clicks day by day; both need `analytics:read`. `GET …/short-links/status` says whether the server has short links (`post:read`), and `PUT …/short-links/settings` sets the workspace's default (`organization:update`). The [command line](/cli#short-links) and the [MCP tools](/mcp#tools) read the same.

## Through the API [#api]

Every endpoint is under `/api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/analytics` and needs the `analytics:read` permission. A range is `days=7`, `30` or `90` (ending today in `timeZone`), or `from` and `to`, at most 366 days, as days run in an IANA `timeZone`.

| Endpoint                                                                        | Answers                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `GET /dashboard?days=30&timeZone=Europe/Paris`                                  | The range and the one before it in one call: totals and followers of each, the same by channel and by platform, each day, and the `topPosts` (5 unless said, 25 at most) |
| `GET /channels/{channelId}?from=2026-11-01&to=2026-11-30&timeZone=Europe/Paris` | One channel day by day: followers, following, posts, views, reach, and what its posts gained                                                                             |
| `GET /export?kind=posts&format=csv&days=90&timeZone=UTC`                        | A file, streamed: `kind=posts` or `accounts`, `format=csv` (RFC 4180, a header line first) or `json` (one object a line)                                                 |
| `GET /summary?from=…&to=…&timeZone=…`                                           | The posts of a range summed up, by channel and day, with the five that did best                                                                                          |
| `GET /posts/{postId}`                                                           | A post's counts on each channel, every reading                                                                                                                           |

A number a platform does not give is `null` in JSON and empty in CSV, never `0`. The same is in the [command line](/cli#analytics) (`peal analytics dashboard`, `channel`, `export`) and the [MCP tools](/mcp) (`analytics_dashboard`, `channel_analytics`). See the [API reference](/reference).
