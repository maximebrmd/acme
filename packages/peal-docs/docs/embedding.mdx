A product of yours can publish to social platforms for its own customers through Peal, without any of them having a Peal account or seeing Peal's app. You hold a **partner account**; each of your customers is a **workspace** it owns; your backend acts in those workspaces with a **partner key**; your customers connect their social accounts on a **hosted page** you send them to; and Peal tells your server what happens through [webhooks](/webhooks).

```
your product ── partner key ──▶ Peal API ──▶ one workspace per customer
your customer ── connect link ─▶ hosted page ─▶ the platform's sign-in ─▶ back to your product
Peal ── webhooks ──▶ your server            (channel.connected, post.published, post.failed…)
```

A workspace is the unit Peal isolates: its channels, posts, media, analytics and webhooks belong to it alone, and deleting it erases all of them. Giving each customer its own keeps one customer's accounts and posts out of every other's reach, by construction rather than by a filter in your code.

## Get a partner account [#account]

A partner account belongs to a workspace of yours, its **home workspace**: the one you signed up with, where your team's members are. The owners and admins of that workspace run the account.

- On Peal Cloud, partner accounts are opened by Peal's team: write to us with what you build.
- On a self-hosted Peal, staff grant one from the [staff console](/staff#partners), on a workspace's page. Setting [`PEAL_PARTNER_SELF_SERVICE`](/environment-variables#sign-in) to `true` lets the owners and admins of any workspace open one themselves, under **Settings**, then **Partner**.

Then, under **Settings**, then **Partner**, in the web app:

1. **Name and logo.** What your customers read on the hosted pages: a name of 80 characters at most, and the HTTPS address of a logo, which their browser loads from your server.
2. **Return origins.** The origins a hosted page may send your customers back to, such as `https://app.example.com`: scheme, host and port, with nothing after, 20 at most. Plain `http://` is taken for `localhost` only, to try the flow in development. A hosted page returns to an address on one of these origins, exactly, and nowhere else.
3. **A partner key.** Its name, what it may do with your workspaces and what it may do inside each. The key is shown once: put it in your backend's secrets.

A partner key starts with `peal_partner_`, and is sent as `Authorization: Bearer peal_partner_…`. Only a signed-in owner or admin creates one, in the web app: no key and no application creates keys. Revoke one there, or with `DELETE /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/partner/keys/{partnerKeyId}`; it stops working at once.

| A key's scope       | What it allows                                        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `workspaces:read`   | List and read the workspaces the partner account owns |
| `workspaces:write`  | Create them, rename them, change their external id    |
| `workspaces:delete` | Delete one, with everything in it                     |

Inside each workspace it owns, a partner key holds the [permissions](/scheduling#roles) you ticked, among those a product needs for its customers: `channel:*`, `media:*`, `post:*`, `analytics:read`, `engagement:read` and `engagement:reply` (the [comments](/inbox) under a customer's posts, and answering them in public as its account: other people wrote those comments, so give a key these only if your product shows or answers them), `assistant:use`, `webhook:*`, `billing:read` and `activity:read`, which reads the workspace's [activity log](/settings#activity): what your key asked to change there, and the accounts connected through connect links. It never holds what belongs to people: members, invitations, API keys, billing changes.

### What a partner key reaches [#isolation]

| With a partner key                                         | Answer                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| A workspace its partner account owns                       | What its permissions allow  |
| A workspace another partner owns                           | 404, as if it did not exist |
| Any other workspace, your home workspace included          | 404                         |
| `/api/me`, members, invitations, API keys of any workspace | 403                         |

The other way round, an API key of a workspace, a member's session or an OAuth token never calls `/api/partner`: 403.

[Two-step verification](/settings#require-two-step) concerns people: partner keys and your customers' workspaces, which have no member, are not affected by it, while your home workspace's owners may require it of its members like any workspace's.

## Create a workspace for each customer [#workspaces]

When a customer signs up with you, create their workspace, with **your own id for them** as its `externalId`. Peal keeps it, finds the workspace by it, and writes it in every [webhook](#webhooks) of that workspace, so you keep no table mapping your ids to Peal's.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.example.com/api/partner/workspaces \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PEAL_PARTNER_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: signup-cus_42" \
  -d '{"name": "Boulangerie Martin", "externalId": "cus_42"}'
```

```typescript
import { PealClient } from "peal-sdk";

const peal = new PealClient({
  token: process.env.PEAL_PARTNER_KEY,
  baseUrl: "https://api.example.com",
});

const workspace = await peal.partner.createWorkspace({
  name: "Boulangerie Martin",
  externalId: "cus_42",
  "idempotency-key": "signup-cus_42",
});
// workspace.id is what every other call takes as workspaceId
```

```python
import os
from peal import Peal

peal = Peal(token=os.environ["PEAL_PARTNER_KEY"], base_url="https://api.example.com")

workspace = peal.partner.create_workspace(
    name="Boulangerie Martin",
    external_id="cus_42",
    idempotency_key="signup-cus_42",
)
```

A creation is safe to send again, two ways:

- **By `externalId`.** It is unique among your workspaces. A second workspace with the same one answers 409 `ExternalIdTaken`, whose `workspaceId` is the workspace that has it: a signup retried never makes two.
- **By `Idempotency-Key`.** Within 24 hours, the same key with the same body answers the workspace the first request made, and makes no other; the same key with another body answers 409 `IdempotencyKeyReused`.

| Request                                        | What it does                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/partner`                             | The partner account, and what it holds: workspaces and channels, now and at the end of each of the last 35 days                             |
| `GET /api/partner/workspaces`                  | Your workspaces, newest first, each with its number of channels: `externalId` finds one exactly, `name` searches, `limit` and `before` page |
| `POST /api/partner/workspaces`                 | Creates one                                                                                                                                 |
| `GET /api/partner/workspaces/{workspaceId}`    | Reads one                                                                                                                                   |
| `PATCH /api/partner/workspaces/{workspaceId}`  | Changes its name or its external id (`null` removes it)                                                                                     |
| `DELETE /api/partner/workspaces/{workspaceId}` | Deletes it, with everything in it ([Offboard a customer](#offboard))                                                                        |

A list answers `total`, what the filters find in all, and `next`: pass it as `before` for the next page, as it is, until it is null. It is a position in the list, not a workspace's id: it stays good when the workspaces before it are offboarded, and anything else in `before` answers 400.

```typescript
const { workspaces } = await peal.partner.listWorkspaces({
  externalId: "cus_42",
});
```

A workspace you create has no members: nobody signs in to it, and only your keys act in it.

## Let a customer connect their accounts [#connect]

Your customer owns their social accounts, and only they can sign in to them. You create a **connect link** for one platform, send their browser to it, and they come back to your product once the account is connected. They never see a password field of yours, and Peal never sees their password either: the platform's own sign-in page asks for it.

```typescript
const { link, url } = await peal.connectLinks.create({
  workspaceId: workspace.id,
  platform: "linkedin",
  returnUrl: "https://app.example.com/settings/social",
  expiresInHours: 24,
  locale: "fr",
});
// Keep link.id with your customer; redirect their browser to url.
```

```python
created = peal.connect_links.create(
    workspace_id=workspace.id,
    platform="linkedin",
    return_url="https://app.example.com/settings/social",
    expires_in_hours=24,
    locale="fr",
)
# created.link.id, created.url
```

| Field            | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `platform`       | One of the platforms [the instance connects](/channels#connect)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `returnUrl`      | Where the page sends them once done. Its origin must be one of your [return origins](#account), exactly: 422 `ReturnUrlNotAllowed` otherwise.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `expiresInHours` | How long the link works, from 1 to 720 (30 days): 168 (a week) unless given                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `locale`         | `en` or `fr`: the language the page opens in. Left out, the visitor's own.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `channelId`      | Reconnect this channel instead of connecting a new account ([Reconnect](#reconnect))                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `connection`     | The way in, on the platforms that have several: for Instagram, `instagramLogin` or `facebookLogin` ([Instagram](/channels#instagram)); for LinkedIn, `linkedinProfile` (the member's profile) or `linkedinPage` (the company pages they manage) ([LinkedIn](/channels#linkedin)). Left out, the server's first. |

The answer's `url` is shown once: Peal keeps only a digest of the token it holds. Needs `channel:connect`.

### What your customer sees [#hosted-page]

1. **Your name and logo**, and one sentence: you ask to connect their account of that platform, to publish there for them. For Mastodon the page asks for their server, for Bluesky their handle.
2. **The platform's sign-in page**, where they sign in and allow the connection.
3. **A choice**, when the sign-in reached several accounts (the Pages a Facebook user manages, the Instagram accounts linked to them, a Google account's YouTube channels): they tick those to connect.
4. **Back to your `returnUrl`.**

The page is in English or French, shows none of Peal's app, and says the connection is handled by Peal. It is kept out of search engines.

### When they come back [#return]

Peal adds two parameters to your `returnUrl`, over any of the same name:

| Parameter     | Value                                                                |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `peal_link`   | The link's id                                                        |
| `peal_status` | `connected`, `cancelled` (they chose to go back instead) or `failed` |

**Anyone can write those parameters**, so treat them as a hint to look, never as proof: read the link with your key.

```typescript
const link = await peal.connectLinks.get({ workspaceId, linkId });

if (link.status === "completed") {
  // link.channelIds: the channels it connected
}
```

| A link's `status` | Meaning                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `pending`         | It waits to be used. `failure` says how its last sign-in ended, if one failed. |
| `completed`       | It connected the channels in `channelIds`, and opens nothing more              |
| `revoked`         | You took it back                                                               |
| `expired`         | Its time passed                                                                |

Do not wait on the browser alone: a customer may close the tab before coming back. Subscribe to [`channel.connected`](/webhooks#channel-connected), which Peal sends when the account is connected, with the workspace's `workspaceExternalId`.

`DELETE /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/connect-links/{linkId}` revokes a link; `GET …/connect-links` lists a workspace's links, those waiting and those over in the last 30 days.

### Reconnect [#reconnect]

When a platform stops accepting a channel's authorization, Peal sends [`channel.reconnect_needed`](/webhooks#channel-reconnect-needed). Create a link with that channel's `channelId`: its page names the account, and only a sign-in to that same account goes through, so the channel keeps its id, its posts and its analytics.

### What a link is, and is not [#link-security]

- **A link is a credential.** Whoever holds its address can connect an account of theirs to that workspace until it is used, revoked or expired. Send your customer's browser to it from a page they are signed in to, or over a channel you trust; give it the shortest life that fits; revoke those you no longer need.
- **It is used once**, by a sign-in that connects an account. Opening it uses nothing, so a link preview does not spend it.
- **It gives nothing else.** It reads no post, no channel and no token, and reaches no other workspace.
- **Only the browser that started the sign-in completes it.** The platform's answer, replayed in another browser, connects nothing.
- **It returns only where you said.** A `returnUrl` on an origin you did not register is refused when the link is created, so a link never sends anyone to a site of someone else's.
- A link's sign-in can be started 20 times, and a workspace has 100 links waiting at most.

After a connection, show your customer which account was connected (`GET …/channels` gives its handle) so they can tell if it is not the one they meant.

## Post for them [#post]

In a customer's workspace, your key uses the same endpoints as any other client: [media](/media), [posts](/scheduling), [analytics](/analytics). To publish in one call, now or at an instant:

```typescript
const post = await peal.posts.send({
  workspaceId: workspace.id,
  "idempotency-key": "campaign-881-cus_42",
  channelIds: [channelId],
  content: { text: "Fresh bread at 7.", media: [], overrides: [] },
  when: { _tag: "at", at: "2026-11-12T06:00:00Z", timeZone: "Europe/Paris" },
});
```

Peal checks the post against every channel's platform before it takes it, and answers every violation at once ([How refusals read](/agents#refusals)). What goes out is public on the platforms: Peal cannot take it back.

## Hear what happened [#webhooks]

Add one endpoint to your **home workspace** with `scope: "partner"`: it hears the events of every workspace your partner account owns, and your home workspace's own.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.example.com/api/workspaces/$HOME_WORKSPACE_ID/webhooks \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOME_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"url": "https://hooks.example.com/peal", "eventTypes": ["channel.connected", "channel.reconnect_needed", "post.published", "post.failed", "post.unconfirmed"], "scope": "partner"}'
```

A partner key does not act in your home workspace: create the endpoint in the web app's **Settings**, then **Webhooks**, signed in, or with an [API key](/settings#api-keys) of the home workspace that holds `webhook:manage`. An endpoint with `scope: "partner"` in a workspace without a partner account answers 404 `PartnerNotFound`.

Every payload names its workspace twice: `data.workspaceId`, Peal's id, and `workspaceExternalId`, yours, as it was when the event happened (null when you gave none).

```json
{
  "id": "019a6f40-2f6b-7a1c-9d2e-5b4c3a2f1e07",
  "type": "channel.connected",
  "version": 1,
  "timestamp": "2026-11-10T09:14:02.000Z",
  "workspaceExternalId": "cus_42",
  "data": {
    "workspaceId": "7b1f6c1e-2d4a-4f0b-9c3e-5a8d2e6f1b90",
    "channelId": "019a6f40-11aa-7c3d-8e21-9f0b6a5d4c33",
    "platform": "linkedin",
    "reconnected": false
  }
}
```

[Webhooks](/webhooks) tells how deliveries are signed, verified, retried and logged. An endpoint of one customer's workspace, with the default scope, hears that workspace alone.

To mark a customer's post done in your product once, subscribe to [`post.finished`](/webhooks#post-finished): it comes when every channel of the post has its result, with each one's. [`channel.expiring`](/webhooks#channel-expiring) warns, a week ahead at most, that a channel's sign-in cannot be renewed: send your customer a [connect link](#reconnect) before it lapses. When your receiver sits behind a gateway that wants a token, give the endpoint [headers of its own](/webhooks#headers) (`headers`): their values are write-only.

### Staging apart, with a workspace filter [#webhooks-staging]

To keep your staging environment's customers apart from production's, give each environment a partner endpoint of its own and list, in `workspaceIds` (200 at most), the workspaces it should hear: your staging endpoint hears your test customers, and your production endpoint, with no list, every workspace. Your home workspace is heard only when it is listed. Change the list as you add test customers with `PATCH …/webhooks/{endpointId}`. The deliveries of every endpoint, with each one's workspace and your id for it, are in your home workspace's [log across endpoints](/webhooks#log), where the failed ones can be sent again together.

## Offboard a customer [#offboard]

```typescript
await peal.partner.deleteWorkspace({ workspaceId: workspace.id });
```

Deleting a workspace erases, for good, everything it holds: its channels and their tokens (revoked at the platforms that take it), its posts, scheduled ones included, which then go out nowhere, its media and their files, its analytics, its webhook endpoints and its connect links. It answers 409 `WorkspaceBusy` while one of its posts is being published: send it again a moment later. Its external id is free again afterwards.

Your partner account closes, and your home workspace is deleted, only once the account owns no workspace: both answer 409 `PartnerOwnsWorkspaces` until then.

## Plans and limits [#limits]

On an instance that bills, the workspaces your partner account owns have no subscription of their own: they are on **your home workspace's plan**.

- **Channels are counted together.** The plan's channels cover those of your home workspace and of every workspace you own. Connecting one more when they are all taken answers 402 `PlanLimitReached`, and a hosted page says no more accounts can be connected for now.
- **The other limits apply to each workspace**, as the plan gives them: posts scheduled in a month, media stored, the writing assistant's requests a day.
- **Over the plan.** If your plan shrinks under what you hold, nothing is deleted, but until the channels held are back within it, by disconnecting some or taking a larger plan, your workspaces schedule and publish nothing ([A workspace over its plan](/billing#over)).

A self-hosted Peal without billing limits nothing. `GET /api/partner` answers what the account holds each day, and [its usage per customer](#usage) what each workspace used, which is what a price by the workspace or by the connected channel would count.

## Usage per customer [#usage]

One call answers what your partner account used across the workspaces it holds, per customer with your own id for each, over a period of UTC days: for your own billing and dashboards.

```bash
curl "https://api.example.com/api/partner/usage?from=2026-11-01&to=2026-11-30&limit=50" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PEAL_PARTNER_KEY"
```

```typescript
const usage = await peal.partnerUsage.get({
  from: "2026-11-01",
  to: "2026-11-30",
});
// usage.workspaces.workspaces: your home workspace first, then each customer's
```

- **Who reads it.** A partner key with the scope `workspaces:read` at `GET /api/partner/usage`, or your home workspace's owners and admins (`partner:read`) at `GET /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/usage/partner`, which answers the same; any other workspace answers 404 `PartnerNotFound`.
- **The period.** `from` and `to` are UTC days, both included, 92 days at most; by default the 30 days that end today.
- **What it answers.** `current`: the workspaces you own now and the channels connected across them and your home workspace, which your plan's channel limit counts. `days`: for each day, the workspaces and channels as the daily count found them at 23:41 UTC (null for a day without one), and the posts that went out. `totals`: the period's posts by platform, its channel-days and, for each calendar month it touches, the posts on platforms that bill per post.
- **Per workspace.** `workspaces` is a page of them, newest first, in the order and with the cursor of [`GET /api/partner/workspaces`](#workspaces), 50 a page unless `limit` (at most 100) says otherwise: pass `next` as `before` for the next page. Your home workspace comes first, on the first page only, one more than asked, with `home: true`. `current`, `days` and `totals`, which cover the whole account, come with the first page only, and are `null` on the pages after it, which read their own workspaces alone. Each gives its `externalId`, the channels it connected itself now, and over the period its channel-days, posts by platform, requests to the writing assistant, latest media storage and metered posts.
- **A customer offboarded during the period** is no longer listed, and its posts leave the sums, as its counts were erased with it; the daily `workspaces` and `channels` still count it for the days it existed.
- The daily counts are kept {{keep-partner-usage-en}}. Counts only: nothing here is priced, and a self-hosted Peal without billing answers `plan` and `allowances` as null.

## Where things are [#surfaces]

| To                                              | Use                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Set the account up, create and revoke keys      | The web app: **Settings**, then **Partner**                                                                                                                                |
| Create, find, change and delete workspaces      | `/api/partner`, with a partner key; `partner` in the SDKs                                                                                                                  |
| Act inside a workspace                          | `/api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/…`, with the same key; `connectLinks`, `channels`, `media`, `posts`, `analytics` in the SDKs                                                |
| Read the account and its workspaces as a person | `peal partner show`, `peal partner keys`, `peal partner workspaces` ([command line](/cli#partner))                                                                         |
| Read the usage per customer                     | `GET /api/partner/usage`, with the key; `partnerUsage` in the SDKs; `peal partner usage`, the MCP tool `get_partner_usage` and **Settings**, then **Partner**, as a person |
| Make a link by hand for a workspace of yours    | `peal links create` ([command line](/cli#links)), or the MCP tool `create_connect_link` ([MCP](/mcp#tools))                                                                |

The [API reference](/reference) lists every endpoint with its errors. The SDKs are generated from the same contract, in eight languages.

### Without a partner account [#agencies]

Connect links work in any workspace: a member who may connect channels, or an API key with `channel:connect`, creates one to hand to whoever owns an account, such as an agency's client. Such a link takes no `returnUrl`: its page ends by saying the account is connected.

## Not there yet [#later]

- **Your own account picker.** The choice among several accounts happens on Peal's hosted page.
- **Your own platform apps.** Your customers authorize Peal's app on each platform, whose name the platform's consent screen shows.
- **Your own domain** for the hosted pages, and pages without Peal's name.
