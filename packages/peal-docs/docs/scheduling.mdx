A post has one text, which each of its channels may replace with its own, and a time it goes out, kept in the time zone you scheduled it in. Write it in the composer, then find it on the calendar.

## Who can do what [#roles]

| Role        | Posts                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owner       | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Admin       | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Editor      | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Contributor | Write and save drafts, and discard their own; cannot schedule |
| Viewer      | Read                                                          |

Cancelling a post, a draft included, taking it back to draft, reposting it and saying whether it went out take the same right as scheduling it. A post's author is the member who created it. Every role but Viewer may also ask the [writing assistant](#assistant).

A workspace can ask that posts wait for an [approval](/review) before they are scheduled: contributors may then ask for a post to go out, and a post that waits shows as **Awaiting approval**.

## Write a post [#write]

From a workspace's card on **Workspaces**, choose **New post**.

1. Under **Channels**, tick the accounts the post goes to. The list holds the workspace's [channels](/channels).
2. Write in **Text for all channels**. Mentions, hashtags and links stand out as you type, and are kept as plain text.
3. To say something else on one channel, open its tab and write its own text there. Left empty, a channel uses the text for all channels.
4. Under **Images and videos**, attach images or videos from the media library, or upload them there: see [Images and videos](/media).
5. When a channel's platform asks for more, such as a YouTube video's title, fill in its fields under **Platform options**: see [Platform options](#options).
6. Choose **Save as draft** to keep the post without a time. It joins the workspace's **Drafts**, which the calendar does not show.

Under **Length on each channel**, each channel the post goes to shows how much room its platform leaves, and what else the platform would refuse, its images and videos included. Platforms count differently, so the same text can fit on one and not on another:

| Platform                | Text limit                                                                                                                                                      | Images and videos       |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Bluesky                 | 300 characters, and 3,000 bytes                                                                                                                                 | 4                       |
| Mastodon                | 500 characters (the default of a server)                                                                                                                        | 4                       |
| X                       | 280, where a link counts 23, with or without `https://` (`peal.social` too), and an emoji or a character outside the Latin, Greek and Cyrillic scripts counts 2 | 4                       |
| Threads                 | 500 characters, where an emoji counts its bytes, 4 for most; 5 different links                                                                                  | 20                      |
| LinkedIn                | 3,000 characters                                                                                                                                                | 20                      |
| Instagram               | 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, 20 mentions; an image or a video is required                                                                                     | 20                      |
| TikTok                  | 2,200, where most emoji count 2 (UTF-16 code units); an image or a video is required                                                                            | One video, or 35 photos |
| YouTube                 | A description of 5,000 bytes, without `<` or `>`; a video is required                                                                                           | 1                       |
| Pinterest               | A description of 800 characters; an image or a video is required                                                                                                | One video, or 5 images  |
| Google Business Profile | 1,500 characters                                                                                                                                                | Photos only             |

Bluesky counts what a reader sees as one character, so an emoji made of several code points counts once. YouTube counts bytes, so an accented letter counts two. TikTok takes 2,200 for a video's caption and 4,000 for a photo post's description: Peal holds both to 2,200. Facebook's limit is the figure commonly reported, as Meta names none; so are Google Business Profile's 1,500 characters, and the 58 of an event's or an offer's title, as Google's reference and help pages name none. Peal checks the limits when you schedule a post, not while you write a draft.

## Threads [#threads]

A post can have parts that follow it. Under **Thread**, choose **Add a part**: each part has its own text, its own images and videos, a counter for every channel, and, if you like, a wait after the part before it, from a minute to a day. Move a part up or down, or remove it, with its buttons. A post has 24 parts after its first at most.

On the platforms with threads, each part goes out as a reply to the part before it:

| Platform | A post's parts become                                                |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| X        | A thread: each part replies to the one before it                     |
| Bluesky  | A thread: each part names the thread's first post and its own parent |
| Mastodon | A thread: each part replies to the one before it                     |

On X, whether a thread goes out depends on the access the server's app has: X documents that an app on self-serve access may reply only under a post whose author mentioned or quoted the replying account. If X refuses the second part, the post is partly published, with the reason that X does not allow this app to post replies, and cannot be posted again from Peal; whoever runs the server can turn X threads off ([`X_THREADS`](/environment-variables)), and X then takes a post alone.

Each part follows the rules of a post on its platform: its length, counted as the platform counts it, and its images and videos. The other platforms take a post alone: a post with parts is refused there when you schedule it, and the composer says so as soon as you tick such a channel. Comments under a post, on the platforms that take them, are not sent yet.

Parts go out in order, each as its own request to the platform:

- nothing is sent after a part that **failed**. The channel reads **Failed**, its page lists the parts that are live, with their addresses, and **Post again** sends the part that failed and those after it, never a part that is live;
- nothing is sent after a part Peal **cannot confirm**. The channel reads **To check**: look on the platform, then say what you found. When that part is live and others were left, the channel reads **Failed** with the reason **Later parts not sent**: a part you found by hand has no id Peal knows, so the next part cannot reply to it. Peal offers no **Post again** there, and the API refuses a repost (409 `target_status_conflict`): post the rest on the platform yourself, as replies to the last part that is live. The same holds when the part to reply to is gone from the platform;
- **Cancel** stops the parts not yet sent. A part that waits is not sent once its wait ends, and the parts that went out stay out.

The post's address is its first part's, and [analytics](/analytics) reads the counts of the first part.

## Platform options [#options]

Some platforms ask each post for more than its text and media. When the post goes to a channel on one of them, **Platform options** shows that channel's fields. A draft keeps them as they are, unfinished; scheduling needs those marked required, and shows under each field what its platform would refuse.

| Platform                | Options                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| YouTube                 | **Title** (required: 100 characters at most, without `<` or `>`), **Made for kids** (required, with no default), **Visibility** (public unless you choose otherwise), **Tags** (500 characters in all), **Category number**, **Notify subscribers** (on), altered or synthetic content, and [more](#media-options)                                      |
| TikTok                  | **Who can view this post** (required, with no default), **Allow comments**, **Allow Duet** and **Allow Stitch** (off until you turn them on), commercial content disclosure, AI-generated content, and [more](#media-options)                                                                                                                           |
| LinkedIn                | **Who can see this post** (anyone unless you choose otherwise; connections only is for a profile), **Keep others from resharing this post**, **Pages to mention** (company pages, each written `@Name` in the text), **Link to show as a card** with its **Link title** (required with a link) and **Link description**, **Document title** (for a PDF) |
| Pinterest               | **Board** (required, one of the account's), **Board section**, **Title** (100 characters at most), **Link** (where a click on the Pin leads, starting with `http://` or `https://`), **Video cover, at second** (the first frame unless you choose), **Dominant colour** (such as `#6E7874`)                                                            |
| Instagram               | A reel's settings and **Collaborators** (see [below](#media-options))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Facebook                | A video's **Video title** (see [below](#media-options))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Google Business Profile | **Post type** (an update unless you choose an event or an offer), **Title**, **Starts** and **Ends** (required for an event or an offer; a title of 58 characters at most), **Button** and its **Button link** (on an update or an event), **Coupon code**, **Link to redeem the offer** and **Terms and conditions** (on an offer)                     |

The post's text is the YouTube video's description, the TikTok post's caption, the Pinterest Pin's description, the LinkedIn post's commentary and the Google Business Profile post's description. Tags are separated by commas; YouTube counts the commas, and two more for a tag with a space in it.

On a Google Business Profile location, the post's text is the post's description. An event's or an offer's dates are on the location's own clock, whatever your time zone, and the end comes after the start. Every button opens its link, which starts with `https://` or `http://`, except **Call now**, which rings the number on the profile and takes no link. An offer shows Google's own button.

To disclose commercial content on TikTok, turn on **Disclose commercial content**, then say whose brand the post promotes: **Your brand** (labelled "Promotional content"), **Branded content** for a third party (labelled "Paid partnership"), or both. Branded content cannot be visible to you only.

On a TikTok channel, the composer reads the account's settings on TikTok, as TikTok's guidelines ask: it names the account the post goes out as, offers only the audiences the account has (a public account: everyone, friends, only me; a private one: followers, friends, only me), greys out the interactions its owner turned off in TikTok, leaves out Duet and Stitch on photo posts, and says how a disclosure labels the post. Scheduling checks those settings again, with the longest video the account may post; TikTok's consent line sits under the options, and posting agrees to it.

On a Pinterest channel, the composer reads the account's boards on Pinterest, up to 500 of them, and, once you chose one, its sections. A board deleted since, or past those read, stays chosen and shows as not in the list; when Pinterest does not answer, **Try again** reads the list again. Peal creates no board: make one on Pinterest first. A Pin has one alt text, the first file's, and a carousel's images share the Pin's title, description and link.

### Mastodon, Bluesky, Threads and X [#feed-options]

None of these options is required. Each is sent only when you choose it, and in a [thread](#threads) it goes either with every part or with the first part alone, as the last column says. Through the API, each option is named as in the second column.

On **Mastodon**:

| Option                          | API name         | Values                                                                     | Left out              | Parts      |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------- |
| **Who can see this post**       | `visibility`     | `public`, `unlisted` (quiet public), `private` (followers only)            | The account's default | Every part |
| **Content warning**             | `contentWarning` | One line, 500 characters at most                                           | None                  | Every part |
| **Mark the media as sensitive** | `sensitive`      | Yes or no                                                                  | The account's default | Every part |
| **Language**                    | `language`       | A code of two or three lower-case letters, such as `fr`                    | The account's default | Every part |
| **Who can quote this post**     | `quotePolicy`    | `public`, `followers`, `nobody`; only with `visibility` public or unlisted | The account's default | Every part |

Mastodon counts a content warning in the post's character limit, with the text: on a Mastodon channel the 500 characters are the warning and the text together, on every part, and the composer counts them so. A content warning also marks the post sensitive, whatever **Mark the media as sensitive** says. Private mentions are not offered: Peal publishes posts. Servers older than Mastodon 4.5 ignore the quote policy, and nobody may quote a followers-only post, whatever it says.

On **Bluesky**:

| Option                                                                          | API name                                             | Values                                                                                                             | Left out   | Parts                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Languages**                                                                   | `languages`                                          | Up to three language tags, such as `en` or `pt-BR`                                                                 | None named | Every part                        |
| **Content label**                                                               | `contentLabel`                                       | `sexual`, `nudity`, `porn`, `graphic-media`                                                                        | No label   | Every part with images or a video |
| **Who can reply**                                                               | `replies`                                            | `everyone`, `nobody`, or `limited` to those of `replyFollowers`, `replyFollowing` and `replyMentioned` that are on | Everyone   | The thread                        |
| **Your followers**, **Accounts you follow**, **Accounts mentioned in the post** | `replyFollowers`, `replyFollowing`, `replyMentioned` | Yes or no, each; with `replies` limited, one at least                                                              | No         | The thread                        |
| **Keep others from quoting this post**                                          | `disableQuotes`                                      | Yes or no                                                                                                          | No         | Every part                        |

A label describes the images or video: a part without media is sent without it. Who can reply and quoting are written with the post, in one request, so a post never goes out without them. They need the account to have been connected since Peal asks for them: on a channel connected before, scheduling refuses them, and its [capabilities](/api-overview#capabilities) say so (`optionNeedsReconnect`). Reconnect the account on the **Channels** page to use them; nothing else changes. In the composer, such a channel offers neither and says so. When Peal could not read the channel's scopes and the account's server then refuses the gates, the post is not sent: the channel reads **Failed**, with the code `optionRefused`, and Peal does not send it again. Reconnect the account, or leave the gates out, and post it again.

On **Threads**:

| Option            | API name         | Values                                                                                           | Left out                                                |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who can reply** | `replyControl`   | `everyone`, `accounts_you_follow`, `followers_only`, `mentioned_only`, `parent_post_author_only` | Everyone                                                |
| **Topic**         | `topicTag`       | One topic on one line, 50 characters at most, without `.` or `&`                                 | None                                                    |
| **Link preview**  | `linkAttachment` | An address starting with `https://`, on a post without images or video                           | Threads previews the first link of a post of text alone |

Threads takes a post alone, so each option is the post's. A link preview on a post with media is refused when you schedule it.

On **X**:

| Option                        | API name             | Values                                                   | Left out                | Parts                             |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Who can reply**             | `replySettings`      | `following`, `mentionedUsers`, `subscribers`, `verified` | Everyone                | The first part                    |
| **Community**                 | `communityId`        | The community's number, digits only                      | The account's followers | The first part                    |
| **Also share with followers** | `shareWithFollowers` | Yes or no; only with a community                         | No                      | The first part                    |
| **Paid partnership**          | `paidPartnership`    | Yes or no                                                | No                      | Every part                        |
| **Made with AI**              | `madeWithAi`         | Yes or no                                                | No                      | Every part with images or a video |

X does not document which accounts may use each of these. When X refuses a post that carries them, and its answer names one of them (the field, or the community), the channel reads **Failed**, with the code `optionRefused`: Peal does not send it again, and X's message is not kept, so it does not say which option X refused. Change the options and post it again. When X refuses such a post for anything else, such as a restricted account, the code is X's own problem type, as for a post without options (`client-forbidden`, or `http403` when X names none), and changing the options will not help.

### YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook [#media-options]

These options are not required either, but YouTube's title and made-for-kids answer and TikTok's audience, above. Each is sent only when you choose it, and some apply only to some media: the composer shows them for those, and scheduling refuses them on another post.

On **YouTube**, besides the options above:

| Option                                    | API name              | Values                                                      | Left out                |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Thumbnail**                             | `thumbnailMediaId`    | A JPEG or PNG image of the [library](/media), 8 MiB at most | The frame YouTube picks |
| **License**                               | `license`             | `youtube` or `creativeCommon`                               | The channel's default   |
| **Embedding allowed**                     | `embeddable`          | Yes or no                                                   | The channel's default   |
| **Public statistics**                     | `publicStatsViewable` | Yes or no                                                   | The channel's default   |
| **Language of the title and description** | `defaultLanguage`     | A language tag, such as `en` or `pt-BR`                     | None named              |
| **Recording date**                        | `recordingDate`       | A day, as `2026-11-27`                                      | None                    |

YouTube sets a thumbnail once the video is uploaded, and only on a channel it allows custom thumbnails. When it does not, or refuses the image, the video is published all the same, with its own thumbnail: the post's page says **The thumbnail was not set**, and through the API its target holds `thumbnailNotSet: true`. Peal sets up to `YOUTUBE_DAILY_THUMBNAILS` thumbnails a day for the whole server (see [environment variables](/environment-variables)), as each draws on the Google project's general quota; past them, a video goes out with YouTube's.

On **TikTok**, besides the options above:

| Option                           | API name                | Values                                                     | Left out        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| **Cover frame, in milliseconds** | `videoCoverTimestampMs` | A video's frame, in milliseconds from its start, within it | The first frame |
| **Photo post title**             | `photoTitle`            | Photos: 90 characters at most, as TikTok counts them       | No title        |
| **Add music automatically**      | `autoAddMusic`          | Photos: yes or no                                          | No              |
| **Cover photo**                  | `photoCoverIndex`       | Photos: the cover's place, from 0 for the first            | The first photo |

A photo post's text is its description, and its title is apart.

On **Instagram**:

| Option                           | API name        | Values                                                       | Left out             |
| -------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| **Format**                       | `format`        | `story` (see [stories and reels](#formats)), or `feed`       | A feed post          |
| **Also share to the feed**       | `shareToFeed`   | A reel: yes or no                                            | As Instagram decides |
| **Cover image**                  | `coverMediaId`  | A reel: a JPEG image of the library, 8 MiB at most           | The cover frame      |
| **Cover frame, in milliseconds** | `thumbOffsetMs` | A reel: its frame, in milliseconds from its start, within it | The first frame      |
| **Audio name**                   | `audioName`     | A reel: one line, 100 characters at most                     | Instagram's name     |
| **Trial reel**                   | `trialReel`     | A reel: `manual` or `performance`                            | Not a trial          |
| **Collaborators**                | `collaborators` | One to three Instagram usernames                             | None                 |

On Instagram, a reel is a feed post of one video; a story takes none of these options. A cover image wins over a cover frame: only the image is sent then. Instagram renames a reel's audio once. A trial reel is shown to accounts that do not follow yours first, and reaches your followers when you share it in the app (`manual`) or by itself when it does well (`performance`). Each collaborator is another account, which receives an invitation to be named as a co-author: name only accounts you mean to invite. Instagram does not document collaborators for accounts connected with Instagram Login: when Instagram refuses such a post, the channel reads **Failed** with Meta's own code. Instagram also shows the alt text of a post's images, up to 1,000 characters, and none on a video.

On **Facebook**:

| Option          | API name     | Values                                                           | Left out    |
| --------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| **Format**      | `format`     | `story` or `reel` (see [stories and reels](#formats)), or `feed` | A feed post |
| **Video title** | `videoTitle` | A video or a reel: one line, 255 characters at most              | No title    |

A thumbnail or a cover is a file of the library that the post does not show: it must be ready and the workspace's, it cannot be deleted while a post that goes out names it, a draft loses it when it is deleted, and a copy of the post leaves it out once it is gone. A channel set or a channel's default options cannot name one.

Through the API, a draft takes its options in `options`, one entry per channel, each tagged by its `platform`; each of the post's targets answers with its own. Updating a draft replaces its options: those left out are removed. A channel added only when the post is scheduled has none yet, so on YouTube, TikTok or Pinterest, give it its options in the draft first.

### Stories and reels [#formats]

On Instagram and Facebook, **Format** chooses what kind of post a channel receives. Peal never guesses it from the file: a vertical video stays a feed post (a reel, on Instagram) unless you choose **Story**, or **Reel** on Facebook.

| Format    | Where     | What it takes                                                                                                                 | The post's text |
| --------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| **Story** | Instagram | One JPEG image of 8 MiB at most, or one MP4 or QuickTime video of 3 to 60 seconds and 100 MiB at most                         | Not sent        |
| **Story** | Facebook  | One JPEG, PNG or GIF image of 10 MiB at most, or one vertical MP4 video (9:16, 540 pixels a side at least) of 3 to 60 seconds | Not sent        |
| **Reel**  | Facebook  | One vertical MP4 video (9:16, 540 pixels a side at least) of 3 to 90 seconds                                                  | Its description |

A story is sent without the post's text: the composer says so under the counters and counts nothing there, and a check through the API answers `textNotSent` for that channel. A story takes no other option (no collaborators, none of a reel's settings, no title) and a story or a reel has no parts after it. A Facebook reel's title is the **Video title**. A story and the feed post of the same file and text are not taken for duplicates of each other; two stories of the same file are, whatever text the posts hold, as a story shows none.

A story disappears after 24 hours. Peal keeps no link to it and reads no statistics or comments of it, and neither a story nor a reel can be boosted from Peal; each counts in the month's posts as any other. Meta asks a reel's and a video story's video to run at 24 to 60 frames a second, which Peal does not check: Meta refuses a video outside it, or below 540p, while it processes it. A reel or a video story reads **Published** only once Meta says it is published: Peal reads Meta's status for up to 10 minutes after sending it. A refusal Meta shows meanwhile reads **Failed** (`processingFailed`). Still not published then, or should Peal not hear whether a reel or a story went out, the channel reads **To check** (`stillProcessing` when Meta was still processing it) and Peal never sends it again: look on the Page, then say what you found. Meta also takes 30 reels a Page within any 24 hours through its API; past them, the reel fails with Meta's code, and you can post it again later.

If Peal's server restarts while a story or a reel is going out, Peal cannot read it back: the channel reads **To check**, for you to check the account, then confirm it or post it again. Peal never sends it again by itself. Through the API, a channel's capabilities list its platform's formats in `postFormats`, each with the media it takes and whether the text is sent.

## Schedule it [#schedule]

Under **When**, pick the **Date**, the **Time** and the **Time zone**, which starts as your browser's. Then choose **Schedule**. It stays off while the post breaks a limit on one of its channels, and the composer lists what to fix. The [platform options](#options) a channel lacks are listed once you choose it, and shown under their fields.

The time is the wall-clock time in that zone, daylight saving included:

- a time the clocks skip, such as 02:30 on the night they go forward, moves to the moment they jump, 03:00;
- a time that happens twice, on the night they go back, is the first one, so the post goes out once.

A time up to a minute ago counts as now. An earlier one is refused: pick a time to come.

## Queues [#queues]

A queue is a weekly timetable of slots in a time zone, such as every weekday at 09:30 and 17:00 in Europe/Paris, for some channels and at most one [channel set](/organise#sets). Instead of choosing a time, add a post to a queue: it takes the queue's next free slot. **Settings**, **Queues** lists the workspace's queues with their next slots, and lets those who may change [channel sets](/organise#roles) add one, change it, delete it or make it the default: owners, admins, editors and contributors (`post:update` through the API). Adding a post to a queue schedules it, so it takes what scheduling that post takes: a contributor's post waits for an approval where the workspace asks for one, and is refused otherwise. A workspace keeps up to 20 queues, each with up to 100 slots.

- A slot is a day and a time of day on the queue's clock, daylight saving included, as a time you choose: a time the clocks skip moves to the moment they jump, and a time that happens twice is the first one.
- A slot is taken while a post the queue placed is scheduled, waiting for an approval or publishing at exactly its time. Nothing else is kept of slots: unscheduling, cancelling or moving such a post by hand frees its slot, and a post of the queue moved onto a slot's time takes it.
- The first queue of a workspace is its **default**, which a post added to no queue in particular goes to. Marking another as the default moves it; deleting the default leaves none until another is marked.
- Changing a queue's slots moves no post: posts already placed keep their times, until you [move them onto the new slots](#reshuffle).
- Deleting a queue leaves its posts scheduled at their times.

In the composer, under **When**, choose **Add to queue**, pick the queue (the default to start with), and read where the post would go: **Next free slot**. Then choose **Schedule**: the slot is decided when Peal receives the post, so two posts added at the same moment never take the same one. A post that names no channel goes to the queue's channels and channel set; one of them disconnected since is left out, and said. From then on the post is an ordinary scheduled post: it is checked against its channels, waits for an approval where the workspace asks for one, shows on the calendar with a queue mark, and can be moved, unscheduled or cancelled. A queued post scheduled already moves to the queue's next free slot the same way.

The plan's posts a month count in the month of the slot found. When that month has no room, the post is refused, as for a time you chose: Peal never skips to a later month by itself. A queue with no slot, or with every slot of the next 366 days taken, refuses the post too.

### Move the waiting posts onto new slots [#reshuffle]

After you change a queue's slots or its time zone, **Settings**, **Queues** offers, while posts wait in it, **Move the waiting posts onto the new slots**, with how many wait. Once you confirm, every post that waits in the queue (scheduled, or waiting for an approval, at a time to come) moves onto the queue's slots as they are now, in the order of their times: the first onto the first free slot, the second onto the next, and so on. Posts moved by hand onto another time move too, as the queue keeps no record of which were. A post already on its slot stays where it is, a slot a publishing post holds is skipped, and what the posts say and where they go do not change. Owners, admins and editors may do it (post:schedule through the API); through the API, a contributor whose posts wait for an approval moves those alone.

Each post moves as if moved by hand, but for approvals: it gets its new time and counts in its new month. A scheduled post goes through the workspace's approvals as if you scheduled it, and a post waiting for an approval keeps waiting, at its new time, whoever moves it: its request stays with whoever asked for it, and nobody is asked again to decide. To schedule such a post yourself, when your own posts need no approval, move it by hand. Peal then says how many posts moved, how many stayed, and how many remain. It stops, keeping what it moved, at a month the plan has no room in, when no slot is left within 366 days, or when someone saves the queue meanwhile: move them again once there is room. Moving them twice moves nothing the second time.

## Repeat a post [#recycling]

A post that is scheduled, or already out, can **repeat**: Peal makes a new post from it at an interval, until a number of posts or a date, saying its text or, in turn, text variants. Open the post and choose **Repeat**: every so many days (1 to 365), weeks (1 to 52) or months (1 to 12), ending after a number of posts (2 to 100, this one counted), on a date, or after 100 posts at most; optionally on a [queue](#queues); with up to 10 **text variants**. Owners, admins and editors may do it (post:schedule through the API); a contributor's posts, where the workspace lets them ask, each wait for an approval. **Settings**, **Repeating posts** lists the workspace's rules with their next three posts, and lets those who may schedule pause, resume or stop one.

- Each new post is an ordinary post, with its own channels' outcome, notifications and analytics: move, unschedule or cancel one as any other, and the rule goes on by its own clock. It is copied from the source with what the workspace still has (its channels still connected, its media, its options, its tags), never with a signature or a default option added since.
- The next post is made once the latest one's time has passed, so the calendar shows one ahead. It is due one interval after the one before, at the same time of day in the source's time zone, daylight saving included; a month shorter than the source's day takes its last day (31 January, then 28 February, then 31 March). On a queue, it takes the queue's first free slot from that time. A time missed while Peal was down is skipped, never made up for. A post whose time, or queue slot, would fall after the rule's last day is not made: the rule ends there, as after its last post.
- The first post says the source's text, the next the first variant, and so on, then the source's again. A variant replaces the text of the post's first part, and its texts on some channels; its media, later parts and options stay. Some platforms refuse a text they already published for the account: variants are for that. Each variant is checked as the source saying it would be when you save it, and again when its post is made.
- A post repeats as whoever started it, with what they may do when it is made: their posts' approvals apply, and a member who left, a key revoked or an application whose access no longer publishes stops it.

Peal stops a rule, rather than skip a post silently, and tells those it concerns ([notifications](/notifications#events)) when its post was deleted, cancelled or taken back to draft, its starter can no longer schedule, none of its channels is left, a platform refuses what it would say, the month has no room in the plan, or its queue was deleted or has no free slot. While its post waits for an approval, it makes nothing and waits. Once that is put right (room in the plan, other variants, a channel connected again, slots on its queue, its post scheduled again), **Resume** sets it going from the next due time after now (with the same variants: other variants for a refused text go with the resume through the [command line](/cli#recycling) or the API), unless another rule repeats the post by then or the workspace has 100 going; for any other end, make the post repeat again. A post has one rule going on at a time, and a workspace 100. A rule keeps the source's first time: moving the source does not move the times it repeats at.

## Post from a feed [#feeds]

A workspace can follow a **web feed** (RSS 2.0, Atom 1.0 or JSON Feed 1.1), such as a blog's: each item that appears in it after you added it becomes a post. **Settings**, **Feeds** adds one from its address (public HTTPS only), with the channels its posts go to (a channel set too, through the [command line](/cli#feeds) or the API), and a **template** its posts are written from: `{title}`, `{link}` and `{summary}` (the item's summary as plain text, 280 characters at most), by default the title, a blank line and the link. **Preview** reads the address and shows its three newest items as the template would write them, keeping nothing.

- **Attach each item's picture** imports the image an item names (its enclosure, its media or its JSON Feed `image`) into the media library, as the person who added the feed, described by the item's title. A picture that cannot be had (not public, too large, not an image, the plan's storage full) leaves it out: the post goes without it, and the feed's items say why.
- By default each item becomes a **draft**, for a person to finish and schedule. Someone who may schedule (post:schedule) can choose a **queue** instead: each item then goes on the queue's next free slot without anyone reading it first, through every rule a post goes through, the approval policy included. What the queue refuses (a platform refuses the text, the month has no room in the plan, the queue has no free slot, a channel is gone, the same post already goes out) becomes a draft, with why. A feed never publishes at once.
- When you add a feed, Peal reads it once and records what it holds: only what appears afterwards becomes a post, and an item dated before the feed was added is never made. Changing its address, or resuming it, does the same.
- Peal reads each feed about once an hour, sends back what the site said of its last answer (so an unchanged feed costs it nothing), and makes at most 3 posts a run and 12 a day per feed: the rest of a flood is skipped, never made later. Each feed's page lists its 50 newest items with what became of each, never what they said.
- A feed acts as whoever added it, as they are when an item is made. Peal stops it, and tells those it concerns ([notifications](/notifications#events)), when its site says it is gone, when it could not be read, or gave something that is not a feed, for seven days, when its starter can no longer make posts there, or when its queue was deleted. **Edit** changes its address, template, channels, mode and queue, the way to put right a channel or a queue gone since: pausing a feed is never refused for them, and resuming one says which to choose again. **Resume** sets it going again, reading it at once; whoever resumes a feed whose starter is gone takes it over, so its posts act as them from then on.
- A site that changes its items' identities (moving its blog, say) makes them all new to Peal: up to three of those dated after the feed was added are posted again that run, and the rest are skipped as too many. Pause the feed before such a move, and resume it after.
- Peal reads a feed's host only once its address checked out as public, on every redirect too; a host whose name answers otherwise between that check and the connection is the one gap left, as Peal's HTTP client cannot connect to the address it checked while keeping the name for TLS.
- What a feed says is other people's text. Peal keeps a digest of each item's identity, never the item, and never gives a feed's text to anything but the posts it makes. A workspace follows 10 feeds at most; adding, moving, resuming and previewing feeds read 30 addresses an hour at most.

## Drafts [#drafts]

From a workspace's card, **Drafts** lists the posts without a time yet, newest first, 20 at a time: each shows the start of its text, its channels and its media, and opens in the composer.

To drop a draft, choose **Discard the draft** in the composer and confirm. It is deleted, and its images and videos stay in the media library. Owners, admins and editors discard any draft; a contributor discards those they wrote.

## Move, unschedule or cancel it [#move-cancel]

Once a post is scheduled, its text is locked; its time is not.

- In the composer, change the date, the time or the zone, then choose **Move to this time**.
- On the calendar, drag the post by its handle to another hour or day. It keeps the time zone it was scheduled in.
- To change what it says, choose **Back to draft**. It leaves the calendar and goes out at no time: edit it, then **Schedule** it again, when it is checked against its channels once more.
- To stop it, choose **Cancel the post** in the composer and confirm. It goes out on no channel, and cannot be scheduled again.

Moving a post does not check its text or its media against its channels again.

## Ask the writing assistant [#assistant]

When the Peal instance has an AI provider, the composer's **Text** section has an **Assistant** menu. It works on the text for all channels, and offers:

- **Write a post…**: say what the post is about, in your own words, and the assistant writes a first text, in the subject's language. Choose a tone if you like, and, when the post goes to a platform with a short limit (X, Bluesky, Threads or Mastodon), the one whose limit it must fit. It is offered whether the text is empty or not, and using its text replaces the one you had. It is told not to add facts, figures, names, dates or links that the subject does not give, and to add hashtags only when the subject asks for them. For another idea, ask again: each one is a request;
- **Rewrite in a tone**: professional, friendly, casual, enthusiastic or witty, keeping the meaning, the language and about the length;
- **Shorten to fit**: one of the platforms of the channels you picked, or, before you pick any, each platform whose limit Peal knows;
- **Translate into**: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Japanese or Chinese (simplified);
- **Suggest hashtags**: up to 8 that the text does not have yet.

When the instance also has an image model, **Choose from the library** offers **Generate an image**, which makes an image from a description into the library, picked for the post: [Generate an image](/media#generate). An image costs 5 of the day's requests.

The suggestion opens in a preview, and nothing changes until you choose **Use this text** or **Add these hashtags**, which adds them on a line of their own; **Discard** drops it. Read it before you use it: the assistant keeps mentions, hashtags and links, and is told not to add facts, but it can still get things wrong.

The assistant takes texts, and subjects, of up to 5,000 characters. Each workspace may send it a number of requests per day, 100 unless the instance sets another, counted from midnight UTC; the menu shows how many are left today. A request that could not reach the AI provider does not count. One that took more than 90 seconds does, as the provider may have worked on it.

Your text, or the subject, goes to the AI provider the instance's administrator chose, and nowhere else. Peal saves neither the text you send nor the suggestion, and never writes them to its logs.

## The calendar [#calendar]

From a workspace's card, choose **Calendar**. It shows the scheduled posts by **Week** or by **Month**, at their times in your browser's time zone, with each post's status. Choose a post to open it in the composer. A day, or an hour of the week that has not started, has a **New post** link that opens the composer with that date and time. Above the calendar, filter the posts by [tag](/organise#tags) and by channel. Times follow your language's clock; to read them on a 12-hour or a 24-hour clock instead, choose **Time format** on your account page: the choice is kept in your browser. To open the calendar on a given week, add `?date=` with a day of that week to its address, as in `?date=2026-11-09`.

## When a post comes due [#publishing]

At its time, Peal sends the post to each of its channels on its own, one post at a time per account, and records how each one ended:

| On a channel | What happened                                                                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Published    | The platform took the post, and gave its address when it has one.                                       |
| Failed       | The platform refused the post, the account needs reconnecting, or the post went out too late, as below. |
| To check     | Peal cannot tell whether the platform published it. Check the platform before posting again.            |
| Cancelled    | The post was cancelled before it went out there.                                                        |

The post as a whole then reads **Published**, **Partly published**, **To check**, **Failed** or **Cancelled**. Its author, and the members who chose to hear about every post, are told how it went on each channel: see [Notifications](/notifications).

## Follow up on a post [#follow-up]

Open a post that went out, from the calendar or a notification: its page shows its text, and for each channel its status, why it failed or is to check, the address of the post on the platform, and every attempt Peal made, with when it tried again. It also says how the post was created and how it was scheduled: in the web app, with an API key, or by an application such as the command line or an MCP client, by name. While the post is going out, the page updates on its own. Members who may schedule posts can then:

- **Post again** on a channel where it failed or is to check: Peal sends it now, as a new send with its own attempts, and the post reads **Publishing** until it has gone. When the post is to check, look on the platform first, as it may be live already;
- **Say what you found** on a channel to check: **It is live**, with the post's address if you have it, or **It is not there**, which fails it there so it can be posted again. Either answer clears it from the posts to check, and marks its notifications as read.

When a platform does not answer the request that publishes the post, Peal asks again only in ways that cannot post it twice: it reads the post back on Bluesky, sends it to Mastodon again under the same key, which Mastodon answers with the post it already made, looks for it among the X account's recent posts, by its text, its links and its files, without ever sending it to X again, asks Threads and Instagram whether the post they were handed was published, looks for it among the Facebook Page's recent posts, by its text, follows the post TikTok already took by the id it gave, asks YouTube's upload how far it got, never uploading the video twice, looks for the Pin among those of its Pinterest board made since, by its title, description, link and kind of media, without ever creating it again, and looks for a post among the Google Business Profile location's posts, by its type, its text and its event's title. It tries three times, over about two and a half minutes. On a LinkedIn company page, it looks for the post among the page's newest, by its text. LinkedIn lets Peal neither read a member's posts nor send one twice safely: on a profile, the post reads **To check** at once. If the server stops in the middle of a post, it checks the same way when it starts again. Only when none of that settles it does the post read **To check**: Peal never posts it again on its own.

Peal waits rather than gives up when a platform is busy:

- a platform that does not answer is tried again after 1, 5, 15, 60 and 60 minutes, then the post fails there;
- a platform that limits how often Peal posts is tried again when it says, up to 8 times and no later than a day after the post's time;
- a platform that no longer accepts the account's access fails the post there, and the channel must be [reconnected](/channels#reconnect).

A post that could not start within an hour of its time, for example because the server was down, is marked missed and does not go out.

An image or video is sent as the platform needs it, and some platforms process videos first: Peal waits up to 15 minutes for them, then tries again later, as for a platform that does not answer.

## Unpublish a post [#unpublish]

A post that went out can be taken back: **Unpublish**, on its page, deletes it from that channel's platform for everyone, every part of a thread, the last first, so a deletion that stops midway leaves a shorter thread that still reads from its start. It cannot be undone, from Peal or by the platform, so Peal asks first, naming the channel. Owners, admins and editors may unpublish (the permission that publishes, `post:publish`), and so may an API key given it, or an application allowed to publish.

The deletion runs in the background, with the channel's other calls, one at a time: the channel reads **Removing…**, then **Removed from** the platform, with when; the post stays **Published** in Peal, as it did go out, so the month's count and the usage do not change, and its address stays, struck through, as a record. A part the platform no longer has, deleted there before, counts as deleted. A platform that does not answer is tried again after 1, 5, 15, 60 and 60 minutes, and one that asks Peal to slow down, when it says; after that the removal **failed**, with why, and **Try again** deletes what is left.

Statistics stop: analytics reads the post no more and keeps the counts it read, and the inbox stops reading its comments and keeps those it has for their 30 days. A post shown by a boost that is not over cannot be unpublished: stop the boost first, as the ad would go on spending on a post that is gone.

Where Peal deletes posts, and where it [edits them](#edit-live):

| Platform                | Unpublish | Edit | Why not                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | --------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mastodon                | Yes       | Yes  |                                                                                        |
| Google Business Profile | Yes       | Yes  |                                                                                        |
| Bluesky                 | Yes       | No   | Bluesky has no call to edit a post                                                     |
| X                       | Yes       | No   | X does not say which posts may be edited, or on which access                           |
| Pinterest               | Yes       | No   | Editing a Pin is in beta, not open to every app                                        |
| LinkedIn                | No        | No   | LinkedIn does not say which permission a deletion or an edit needs                     |
| Facebook Pages          | No        | No   | Meta says only some apps may delete Page posts, and not which permission an edit needs |
| Threads                 | No        | No   | Deleting needs a permission Meta grants after a review; Threads has no call to edit    |
| Instagram               | No        | No   | Deleting needs a permission Meta grants after a review; Instagram has no call to edit  |
| YouTube                 | No        | No   | It needs a wider permission than the one Peal asks for                                 |
| TikTok                  | No        | No   | TikTok's API has no call to delete or edit a post                                      |

Where it cannot, delete the post on the platform itself. A post confirmed by hand cannot be unpublished from Peal either: Peal has no id for it. A channel disconnected, turned off or to reconnect cannot be unpublished on until it is back.

## Edit a published post [#edit-live]

A post that went out can have its text corrected on Mastodon and Google Business Profile: **Edit**, beside the channel on the post's page, opens the text of that channel with the channel's counter, and **Save** replaces it on the platform. Only the text changes: never the media, the options, the audience or the time. The change is public at once, so whoever may unpublish may edit (`post:publish`), but not a member whose posts need an [approval](/review): an approver makes the edit for them, as it would put text nobody reviewed out.

The new text is checked as the channel's text is when a post is scheduled: the platform's length (a Mastodon content warning counts), its characters, and the rest. Once the platform took it, it is that part's own text on that channel in Peal (other channels keep theirs), the channel reads **Edited on** with the date, and webhooks hear `post.edited`. Peal keeps no earlier text; Mastodon shows its own history of edits. The edit runs in the background, with the channel's other calls, and is tried again as a deletion is; after that it **failed**, with why, and **Try again** asks again.

Mastodon is sent the post again with its media, its content warning and its sensitivity as they are on Mastodon at that moment, changes made there since included, as Mastodon clears whatever an edit leaves out, and its language is left as it is; a post with a poll is refused, as the edit would remove the poll. Reading the post first needs the `read:statuses` permission, which channels connected before Peal asked for it lack: reconnect the channel. Each part of a thread is edited on its own, by its place from 0 (the post itself).

## Shorten links [#short-links]

On a server whose operator set where short links live (`PEAL_SHORT_LINK_BASE`), the composer shows **Shorten links** under the text, ticked for new posts when the workspace's settings say so. A post that asks goes out with each link written with `http://` or `https://`, and longer than a short address, replaced by one on each channel: the address redirects to the link and counts each visit. Every channel, and every part of a thread, gets addresses of its own, so you can tell which channel brought visitors; a run that is tried again sends the same addresses. A link without a scheme, a short one, or one naming a user and password is left as written, and the line under the box says how many links will be shortened.

Peal never makes the text longer: a link is replaced only by an address that is not longer than it, so the counters, the previews and the checks made when the post was scheduled all still hold. The text Peal keeps stays as you wrote it; only what the platform receives changes. Pinterest always gets links as written, as its rules refuse link shorteners; so do Instagram and TikTok, whose captions make no link clickable, and a link to a channel's own platform stays as written there, as the platform shows it natively. An edit of a published post sends its text as written, without short addresses.

A short link goes with its workspace: deleting the workspace, or the operator changing or removing the short address, makes the short links in its published posts stop working. Owners and admins choose whether new posts ask for short links by default, in the workspace's **Settings**; whoever writes a post chooses for that post. [Analytics](/analytics#short-links) says how clicks are counted.

## From an agent, the command line or the API [#api]

What the composer does, an [MCP client](/mcp#tools), the [command line](/cli#posts) and the [HTTP API](/api-overview) do too, through the same checks: writing a draft, scheduling it, publishing it now, moving it, taking it back to draft and cancelling it. `POST /api/workspaces/{workspaceId}/posts/send` creates a post and sends it in one request, now or at an instant, and refuses it whole, listing every violation at once, when it breaks a rule of one of its channels' platforms. [Posting through the API](/posting-api) shows how to check a post first, send several at once, import them from a CSV file and list them.

An application you connected schedules and publishes only if you let it: publishing is a permission of its own, which you may leave out so that it writes drafts for you to review. An API key does only what the permissions it was given allow. [Posting from an AI agent](/agents) explains both, with an example.
