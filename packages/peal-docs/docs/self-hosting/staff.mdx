The staff console is for the people who run a Peal server: it finds an account or a workspace, tells how the server is doing, and lets staff act on accounts. It lives at `/admin` on your Peal address (`/fr/admin` in French), and its API under `/api/admin`.

## Make an account staff [#make-staff]

Staff are accounts with Better Auth's admin role. No page grants it: set it in the database, then sign in again, as a session keeps the role it started with.

```sql
UPDATE auth."user" SET role = 'admin' WHERE email = 'you@example.com';
```

To take the role back, set `role` to `NULL`, then have other staff **Sign out everywhere** the account. The sessions it opened to act as someone else end on their next request, whatever it is, as the server checks on each one that their staff member is still staff and not banned, and the audit log records each end.

The console refuses anyone but staff in their own session, signed in or not, answering 404 as if it were not there. An API key, an application acting through OAuth, or staff while they act as someone else are refused too. A banned staff account is refused at once.

<Callout type="info" title="Restrict the console at your edge">
  Peal's own servers also put `/admin`, `/en/admin`, `/fr/admin` and
  `/api/admin` behind Cloudflare Access. Behind your own edge, you may restrict
  them to your team's addresses the same way.
</Callout>

## What the console shows [#screens]

| Page              | What it shows                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| System            | Whether the database answers, the release the server runs (`OTEL_SERVICE_VERSION`), the posts due within the hour, those overdue, and the channels to reconnect in every workspace                                           |
| Accounts          | Accounts by email, name or id, newest first, 25 a page                                                                                                                                                                       |
| An account        | Its name and email, whether the address is confirmed, its language, when it was created and last signed in, its ban, its workspaces and roles, how many sessions it has open, and whether two-step verification is on        |
| Workspaces        | Workspaces by name, slug or id, with their members                                                                                                                                                                           |
| A workspace       | Its members and roles, its plan and subscription, its channels and whether each works, how many posts it has in each status, the latest posts that failed or went unconfirmed, and whether it requires two-step verification |
| Contract requests | The requests to cancel a contract or withdraw from one that wait for staff, oldest first                                                                                                                                     |
| Audit log         | What staff did, newest first                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

Staff see who an account is, never what it wrote: no post's text, no image or video, no token. A failed post shows as its ids, its platform, why it failed and the platform's code. To see what a member sees, staff act as them (see below).

"Last signed in" is when the newest session the account opened itself started, while one is left; sessions staff open to act as it do not count.

## What staff do [#actions]

Each action asks why, and keeps the reason in the audit log with the staff member's name. Write what was asked, not more about the person than needed.

| Action                          | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Ban**                         | For a day, a week, 30 days or until lifted: the account is signed out everywhere, sessions it opened as staff to act as someone else included, and can neither sign in nor use its API keys meanwhile. **Lift the ban** undoes it.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Sign out everywhere**         | Ends every session of the account, on every device, those staff opened to act as it and those it opened as staff to act as someone else included. Its API keys keep working.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Send the confirmation again** | Emails a new link to confirm the address, in the account's language, while it is not confirmed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Reset two-step verification** | While it is on, for a person who lost their phone and their recovery codes: turns [two-step verification](/guides/workspace/account#two-step) off, deletes the authenticator and the recovery codes, forgets the browsers it trusted, signs the account out everywhere, and emails it. They then sign in with their password, and may turn it on again. Owners cannot reset a member's: it is staff's alone. |
| **Act as this account**         | Signs the staff member's browser in as the account for an hour. A banner at the top of every page says whose account it is, with **Stop acting as them**, which brings back the staff member's own session.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

<Callout type="warning" title="Before resetting two-step verification">
  Check who is asking first: an email from the account's address is not proof,
  as the mailbox may be what was taken.
</Callout>

### Acting as an account [#acting-as]

While acting as an account, staff see its posts and media, and can write and edit drafts in its name. What they make carries the account's name, as its author, and the audit log keeps who made it (see below). They can read its notifications, not mark them read. They can ask the writing assistant in its workspaces, which counts toward those workspaces' daily requests.

They cannot schedule or publish, connect channels, manage API keys or billing, change its workspaces or the account, or let an application act for it. They cannot turn its two-step verification on or off, see its key or make recovery codes, and are never asked the second step: the session is theirs, opened from the console; whether a workspace that requires it opens follows the account's own.

Staff never ban, sign out, reset the two-step verification of or act as themselves, and never act as other staff. No one deletes an account but its owner, from [their account settings](/guides/workspace/account#delete-account).

Better Auth's own admin endpoints under `/api/auth/admin/` are off, so nothing staff do bypasses the audit log; only `POST /api/auth/admin/stop-impersonating` stays, which the banner calls.

## Two-step verification for staff [#two-step]

An account's page says whether its two-step verification is on, and a workspace's whether it requires it of its members. With `PEAL_STAFF_TWO_STEP=true` ([Environment variables](/self-hosting/environment-variables)), the console is refused to staff whose own account does not have it on: they are told to turn it on in their account settings, while anyone who is not staff still finds no console.

<Callout type="warning" title="Turn it on last">
  Turn it on once every staff account has it on.
</Callout>

## Partner accounts [#partners]

A partner account lets a product use Peal for its own customers, one workspace each, through the API ([Embedding Peal](/developers/embedding)). A workspace's page says where it stands: whether it has a partner account, with how many workspaces that account owns, or whether a partner owns it, with the partner's home workspace and the id the partner gave it.

**Grant a partner account** opens one for the workspace, named after it, and asks why, as every action does. The workspace's owners and admins then name it, create its keys and register where hosted pages send their customers back, under **Settings**, then **Partner**. With [`PEAL_PARTNER_SELF_SERVICE`](/self-hosting/environment-variables#sign-in) set to `true`, they open one themselves. The audit log records each grant with the workspace.

A workspace a partner owns has no members: staff read it as any other workspace, and nobody can act as a member of it.

## Contract requests [#contract-requests]

On a server that bills, anyone cancels a contract or withdraws from one without signing in ([Plans and billing](/guides/workspace/billing#cancelling)). A request that gives its contract number, or whose sender follows the link emailed to an address that manages a contract, is applied without anyone's help.

**Contract requests** lists the others: those no contract was found for, and those whose sender has not followed their link yet. Each shows what its sender wrote, and nothing else: their name, the address their acknowledgement went to, the contract as they named it, the day wanted, and the account that address signs in to, if any.

| Action                      | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Apply to this workspace** | Ties the request to a workspace's contract, by the workspace's id. It takes effect as of the date the request was received, whenever you apply it: a renewal charged since is refunded, and a withdrawal's 14 days and refund count from that date. The sender is emailed what became of it. |
| **Close without applying**  | For a request sent twice or by someone who holds no contract. The sender is not written to: write to them yourself when they need an answer.                                                                                                                                                 |

<Callout type="warning" title="Check who holds the contract">
  Apply a request only once you know the sender holds that contract, as by
  writing to the address the account has on file.
</Callout>

Both ask why, and are in the audit log with the request's id and the workspace's, never the name or the address the request holds. Requests are kept 5 years, as the contract's orders are.

## Short links [#short-links]

**Short links** finds a [short link](/guides/posts/published#short-links) by its code, or by its whole address pasted as reported: where it leads, the workspace and post that hold it, and whether it is on.

- **Turn it off**, with a reason, when a link is reported as leading somewhere it should not: whoever follows it then gets `410 Gone` and nothing is counted. The post on the platform keeps its text, and **Turn it back on** makes it redirect again.
- A workspace's page lists its short links, newest first, and **Turn them all off** stops every one still on at once, with a reason, recorded once in the audit log.

Each is recorded in the audit log, with the reason, the link's workspace and its code, before it is done. A short link also answers only while the part of the post that holds it is live on its platform (`404` before it went out), and `410` once staff banned every account that owns its workspace.

The console's own endpoints, through the API:

| Endpoint                                              | What it does                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/admin/short-links/{code}`                   | Finds a short link                |
| `POST …/disable` and `…/enable`, with a `reason`      | Turns it off or back on           |
| `GET /api/admin/workspaces/{workspaceId}/short-links` | Lists a workspace's short links   |
| `POST …/short-links/disable`                          | Turns a workspace's links all off |

## API rate limits [#rate-limits]

On a server that bills, a workspace's page shows the [rate limit](/developers/conventions/rate-limits#standing) its keys and the applications acting in it send under: {{api-without-en}} requests a minute without a payment method, the server's setting with one, or a limit of its own. Staff give it one on request, {{api-on-request-en}} usually, from 1 to 10,000, with a reason, and remove it the same way: it applies within a minute, whatever the billing account pays, and goes with the workspace. Each change is recorded in the audit log first (`setRequestLimit`, with the limit). On a server that does not bill, the setting is everyone's, and nothing is set.

| Endpoint                                                        | What it does                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/admin/workspaces/{workspaceId}/request-limit`         | What the workspace sends under now                              |
| `POST …/request-limit`, with `requestsPerMinute` and a `reason` | Sets its own limit, or removes it with `requestsPerMinute` null |

## Refunds that did not go through [#refunds]

On a server that bills, **Billing accounts** lists, newest first, the money Peal owes back that Stripe did not give: a credit of a legacy plan's unused time that Stripe refused for good (the plan was ended all the same), and a refund Stripe reported failed after it was made (its `refund.failed` event: subscribe the webhook endpoint to it). Each shows its amount, Stripe's code, the subscription or the refund and the workspace. Settle each by hand, in Stripe's dashboard or another way: the customer was told the money goes back. The alert `peal-credit-problems` fires on each. `GET /api/admin/billing/credit-problems` answers the same list.

## The audit log [#audit-log]

Each action is recorded before it is carried out, so nothing staff do goes unrecorded: who did it, what, to which account, why, and when, with a ban's end. An action that then failed still shows what staff asked for. The log keeps ids, never what anyone wrote.

| Entry                        | Kept                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any entry                    | 12 months                                                                                                                                       |
| A ban, or the lifting of one | 5 years (the limitation period for a claim about it, art. 2224 of the French Code civil), or 12 months past the end of a ban that lasted longer |
| A ban until lifted           | As long as it holds; it ends when it is lifted or the account is closed                                                                         |

A daily job (`staff.audit-retention`, at 03:41 UTC) deletes older entries.

### While staff act as an account [#impersonation-log]

The server records every request that changes something, before it runs, whichever way it comes: Peal's API (`/api/…`) and the sign-in service's routes (`/api/auth/…`) alike. The handler of the request has no say in it, so a change cannot skip its record, and a change the server then refuses is recorded too. Each entry says:

| Field     | What it holds                                                                                                                   |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Staff     | The staff member who acted                                                                                                      |
| Account   | The account they acted as                                                                                                       |
| Action    | Changed something as the account                                                                                                |
| Operation | The API's endpoint, as `group.endpoint` (`posts.create`, `posts.schedule`), or the sign-in route, as `auth:/path`               |
| Workspace | The workspace it was in                                                                                                         |
| Ids       | The ids the request named, by name (`postId`, `channelId`, `organizationId`); never the text, media or anything else it carried |
| When      | When it was asked for                                                                                                           |

Reads are not recorded. MCP clients, the command line and API keys never act for staff: their tokens carry no impersonation, and staff acting as an account may not let an application act for it or create a key. A request to delete one of its workspaces or close the account is refused, so no subscription is cancelled, and recorded like any other change. These entries are kept 12 months, like the rest of the log.

The log also records how each impersonation ended: **Stopped acting as the account** when staff stop it from the banner or sign out, and **Acting as the account ended, by the server** when the server ends it, as its staff member was banned, signed out everywhere or is no longer staff, or the account was banned or signed out everywhere. One that is never stopped ends an hour after it began, as its start in the log shows.

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem title="When an account is closed">
    The log forgets it as the account acted on: entries about it lose its id,
    the reason given, the workspace and the ids a change named, and its id
    leaves every other entry that names it.

    A ban, or the lifting of one, keeps its reason, with a keyed pseudonym of
    the account's email address instead of its id, for its 5 years: whoever
    runs the server may have to show a court what it did and why, and can find
    the entry again only from that address.

    Entries it made as staff keep their author, as a legitimate interest:
    whoever runs the server answers for what staff did to other people's
    accounts, after staff leave too. What was done, and when, stays.

    The account's data download lists what staff did to it, with the reason
    given and the changes staff made as it (what, where and on which ids), but
    not who did it.

  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Legal requests [#legal-requests]

A judicial authority may ask, in writing, for the data that identifies who contributed content (loi n° 2004-575, Article 6, V, B). A server that keeps it ([Legal retention](/self-hosting/privacy#legal-retention)) answers from **Legal requests** in the console (`/admin/legal`), which staff see only with the legal role too. Other staff get the not-found page there, and 404 from its API, like anyone else.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Give the legal role">
    Give it to the few people who answer authorities, and no one else, then
    have them sign in again:

    ```sql
    UPDATE auth."user" SET role = 'admin,legal' WHERE email = 'you@example.com';
    ```

  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the request from the authority's document">
    - What it names: an email address (the closed accounts that had it), an account's id (closed or not), or a workspace's id.
    - The authority, the request's reference, and its legal basis, the text that gives the authority the power to ask.
    - What it asks for, and nothing more: who held the account, how it signed in, its payment references, the connections that created, changed or deleted content, and the period of those connections when the request names one.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the request and show the answer">
    **Record the request and show the answer** records it first, then shows
    only what the request covers: the closed accounts that match, and the
    connections of those accounts, of the account named, or in the workspace
    named. A category the request did not ask for reads **Not covered by this
    request**, and the server sends none of it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

How an account signed in and paid is kept {{keep-legal-account-en}} after it closed, who held it {{keep-legal-identity-en}}, and each connection {{keep-legal-connections-en}}. This is the only way to read what a closed account left. A live account's details are on its page in the console; only the connections need a legal request.

Each answer is recorded, before it is given, with the staff member, the authority, its reference, its legal basis, what was asked about and for, and how many records it returned, never the data handed over, and the record is kept {{keep-legal-disclosures-en}}. **Answers given** lists the records, newest first, and each shows in the audit log too, as **Answered a legal request** with the authority and its reference, so the rest of staff see it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Tell the people you give the role to">
  The page says before anything is entered that answers are recorded: tell the
  staff you give the role to that their answers are recorded and reviewed, as
  employment law may require of you. Enter only a request an authority made in
  writing, and keep the request itself with your legal records.
</Callout>

The same is available to scripts, as staff in their own session: `POST /api/admin/legal-requests` answers a request, and `GET /api/admin/legal-requests` lists the answers given.
