Change your name, language and password, secure your sign-in, download your data or delete your account. Your account is yours, across every workspace you belong to.

## Your account [#account]

Choose **Account settings** in the account menu.

| Setting                    | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**                   | The name other members see, and that your invitations sign with.                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Language**               | English or French. Peal writes your emails in it, switches the app to it as soon as you save, and opens in it whenever you sign in.                                                                                                                       |
| **Password**               | Your current password confirms it is you. Changing it signs your other sessions out, unless you untick that.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Sessions**               | The browsers and devices signed in to your account, with when they signed in and were last active. Sign one out, or every session but the one you use.                                                                                                    |
| **Connected applications** | The [command line](/developers/cli) and the [MCP clients](/developers/mcp) you let act for you, with what they may do and in which workspaces. **Revoke** one, and it can no longer renew its access: the access it already holds ends within 15 minutes. |

### Two-step verification [#two-step]

With two-step verification on, signing in asks, after your password, for a code from an authenticator app on your phone. Your email address must be confirmed first.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn it on">
    Under **Two-step verification**, **Turn on** asks for your password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan the QR code">
    Peal shows a QR code to scan with the app, and its key as text to type in
    instead. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the recovery codes">
    Peal then shows ten recovery codes, once: copy or download them, and keep
    them somewhere safe. Each one works once, in place of a code, when you do
    not have your phone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Turning it on does not sign out the browsers and applications signed in before: sign them out from **Sessions** if you wish.

At sign-in, after your password, enter the app's code, or choose **Use a recovery code**.

- **Trust this browser for 30 days** skips the second step on that browser until then, or until two-step verification is turned off, which forgets every browser trusted.
- After five wrong codes, sign in again with your password. After ten in a row, two-step verification is locked for 15 minutes.
- The second step expires 10 minutes after the password.

The section says how many recovery codes are left.

- **New recovery codes** replaces them all, with your password.
- **Turn off** asks for your password too, and is refused while a workspace you belong to [requires it](/guides/workspace#require-two-step): the section names those workspaces.

Peal emails your address each time it is turned on or off, new codes are made, or a recovery code is used to sign in. If it was not you, change your password.

If you lose your phone and your recovery codes, write to whoever runs the server. Their staff can [reset it](/self-hosting/staff#actions) once they know it is you, which signs you out everywhere. Sign in with your password, then turn it on again. Codes by email or text message are not offered: an emailed code protects nothing when the mailbox is what was taken.

### Download your data [#export]

**Download my data** saves a JSON file of everything Peal keeps about you:

- your account, how you sign in, and your sessions, with their browser and address (not those staff opened to act as you, which are theirs);
- your memberships, and the invitations you sent or received;
- your connected applications, your notifications and your email preferences, including whether you hear about every post;
- the changes you asked for in workspaces, as their [activity logs](/guides/workspace#activity) keep them: when, which operation, how you signed in and the status answered, never what a request carried;
- what the server's staff did to your account (a ban, a confirmation sent again, acting as you, and each change they made while acting as you, with where and on which ids…), when, and the reason they gave, but not who did it;
- the posts you wrote in the workspaces you share, with where they went, the options each platform got, and whether YouTube kept a video private;
- the workspaces where you are the only member, with their channels, posts, [tags, signatures, channel sets and default options](/guides/posts/organise), [queues](/guides/posts/queues#queues), analytics (each post's counts, as Peal read them), media, API keys, writing assistant usage and billing: their plan, how each of their subscriptions stands, and how many posts they made each month on X, with and without a link. Each image or video the store holds comes with a link that downloads it for 7 days;
- what the law has the server keep beyond your account (`legalHold`): each connection that created, changed or deleted content in the past year (when, from which address, over which protocol, and how it signed in), what closing your account would leave and for how long, why, and who may obtain it. On a server that keeps nothing for the law, it is `null`.

The rest of a workspace you share belongs to its team: the file holds your membership of it and the posts you wrote there, not the others' posts, its channels, its media or its analytics. It holds no password, token, key or card. Payment details and invoices stay with Stripe, in the billing portal.

Each time a copy is downloaded, from the web app or the [command line](/developers/cli/workspace#account), Peal emails your address to say so. If it was not you, change your password and sign out your other sessions.

### Delete your account [#delete-account]

**Delete my account…** asks for your password, then deletes the account for good. As when signing in, after three passwords tried within 10 seconds, wait a few seconds before trying another.

<Callout type="danger" title="Deleting your account cannot be undone">
  The workspaces where you are the only member go with everything in them, as
  [deleting a workspace](/guides/workspace#delete-workspace) does.
</Callout>

- You leave the other workspaces. The posts you wrote there stay, without your name, as do the channels you connected, which belong to the workspace.
- Connections you had started and not finished, your sessions, notifications, preferences, invitations and connected applications go too.
- A confirmation goes to your address, in your language.

While you are the only owner of a workspace that other members use, the account cannot be deleted: Peal lists those workspaces. Make another member an owner of each, or delete them, first.

When a workspace it would delete has a subscription Stripe may still bill, the dialog says what that ends and waits until you tick **Cancel the subscription now**, as [deleting a workspace](/guides/workspace#delete-workspace) does. Those subscriptions are cancelled at once, without a refund of the period paid for, and the confirmation email says so.

#### What the law keeps [#legal-hold]

On Peal Cloud, and on any server whose operator French law obliges, only what that law has a host keep is left, apart from everything else and read only to answer a judicial authority's written request:

| What is kept                                                                                       | For how long                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Your name and email address                                                                        | {{keep-legal-identity-en}}                      |
| How you signed in (each sign-in method's identifier, never your password) and your Stripe customer | {{keep-legal-account-en}}                       |
| Each connection that created content                                                               | {{keep-legal-connections-en}} after it was made |

A daily job then deletes them. The dialog and the confirmation say so when the server keeps them. On any other server, nothing is left ([Self-hosting](/self-hosting/privacy#legal-retention) says which operators must keep them).
