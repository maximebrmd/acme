Peal serves the Model Context Protocol (MCP), so an AI assistant, or any other MCP client, can work in a workspace for you: read its posts, analytics, billing and members, write drafts, follow up on posts that went out, and invite people. The client signs in as you, in a browser, and acts in the one workspace you pick, with no more than your role allows.

## The endpoint [#endpoint]

The endpoint is `/api/mcp` at the address you open Peal at, such as `https://peal.example.com/api/mcp`. It speaks Streamable HTTP, in the protocol revisions 2026-07-28, 2025-11-25 and 2025-06-18.

`peal mcp`, from the [command line](/cli#mcp), prints the endpoint and a configuration most MCP clients take:

```json
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "peal": {
      "type": "http",
      "url": "https://peal.example.com/api/mcp"
    }
  }
}
```

Give the client this address, or this configuration, as its documentation says for a remote server over HTTP.

## Sign the client in [#sign-in]

Peal signs MCP clients in with OAuth. You give the client no key: on its first request, Peal answers that it needs a token, and tells it where to ask for one.

1. The client registers itself with Peal, then opens a browser on Peal's sign-in page. Sign in if you are not signed in yet.
2. **Pick a workspace.** The client acts in that one workspace only. To use it in another, connect it again and pick the other one.
3. Peal shows what the client asks for, and where it sends you back afterwards. Check both, then choose **Allow**, or **Deny**. Peal does not check who made a client that registered itself, and says so: allow one only if you trust it and started the connection yourself.

Back in the client, its tools work. Peal remembers your answer for that client and workspace, so connecting it again skips the question.

<Callout type="note" title="If the client cannot register">
  Most MCP clients register themselves before they sign in, which a Peal server
  allows only when its administrator turns it on (`MCP_DYNAMIC_REGISTRATION`,
  see [Self-hosting](/self-hosting#mcp)). `peal mcp` warns when it is off.
</Callout>

## What the client may do [#scopes]

The consent page lists what the client asks for:

| Permission                                                 | What it allows                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Know that it is you                                        | Your account's id                                                |
| See your name and picture, see your email address          | Your profile                                                     |
| Stay connected while you are away, until you disconnect it | Renew its access without you                                     |
| Read the workspace: posts, channels, media and analytics   | Read, and change nothing                                         |
| Do what your role allows in the workspace                  | Anything your [role](/scheduling#roles) allows, reading included |

A client that has neither of the two workspace permissions can do nothing. Its access lasts 15 minutes at a time, and the client renews it on its own. Every request checks that you still belong to the workspace and are not banned.

## Tools [#tools]

Each tool acts as you, in the workspace the client signed in to, through the same checks as the web app: your role decides what it may do, and a client you let read only is refused every change, with a message saying so. To let it change things, connect it again and allow it to do what your role allows.

These tools read, and change nothing:

| Tool                 | What it answers                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `whoami`             | You, and the workspace the client acts in, with your role and what the client may do there                                                                                       |
| `list_workspaces`    | Your workspaces and your role in each, marking the one the client acts in                                                                                                        |
| `list_channels`      | The workspace's [channels](/channels), with whether each one posts or must be reconnected. Never their tokens.                                                                   |
| `list_drafts`        | The workspace's drafts, newest first, a page at a time                                                                                                                           |
| `get_post`           | A post: what it says, when it goes out, and how it went on each channel, with the ids of its targets, their platform options, and why one failed                                 |
| `get_tiktok_creator` | A TikTok channel's settings, fresh from TikTok: who it posts as, the audiences a post may choose, the interactions turned off, its longest video. It reads TikTok, outside Peal. |
| `analytics_summary`  | The counts of the posts published over a range of days, in a time zone, as [Analytics](/analytics) shows them                                                                    |
| `post_analytics`     | A post's counts on each channel it went out on, over time                                                                                                                        |
| `get_billing`        | The workspace's plan, its limits, how much of each the workspace uses, and every plan with its prices                                                                            |
| `list_members`       | The workspace's members, with their name and role, as every member sees them; their email addresses only with `includeEmails`                                                    |
| `list_invitations`   | The pending invitations, for those who may invite; invitations to the owner role for owners only                                                                                 |
| `server_health`      | Whether the Peal server is up and reaches its database                                                                                                                           |

Two more read [what a channel takes](/api-overview#capabilities), so a client need not know each platform's rules. They change nothing, and may ask the channel's platform, outside Peal (`openWorldHint`):

| Tool                       | What it answers                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `get_channel_capabilities` | How a channel's text is counted and limited, the media it takes, its platform's options field by field, its lookups, whether Peal reads its posts' counts, and what the account itself restricts    |
| `run_channel_lookup`       | A [lookup](/api-overview#lookups) of a channel: account data its options refer to, such as the categories a YouTube video can be filed under or the boards of a Pinterest account, a page at a time |

These tools change something. Each one tells the client, through MCP's tool annotations, whether it destroys or overwrites (`destructiveHint`), whether calling it again changes nothing more (`idempotentHint`), and whether its effect reaches outside Peal (`openWorldHint`), so a client can ask you before it calls one:

| Tool                | What it does                                                                                                                                                                          | Destroys or overwrites | Again changes nothing | Outside Peal |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------- | ------------ |
| `create_draft`      | Saves a new draft, with the [platform options](/scheduling#options) of its YouTube, TikTok and Pinterest channels. It goes out at no time: scheduling stays with you, in the web app. | no                     | no                    | no           |
| `update_draft`      | Replaces a draft's text, media and channels with those given. Its options stay on the channels it keeps unless the call gives others.                                                 | yes                    | yes                   | no           |
| `discard_draft`     | Deletes a draft for good.                                                                                                                                                             | yes                    | yes                   | no           |
| `unschedule_post`   | Takes a scheduled post back to draft: it loses its time.                                                                                                                              | yes                    | yes                   | no           |
| `repost_target`     | Publishes a post again, now, on one channel where it failed or is unconfirmed.                                                                                                        | no                     | no                    | yes          |
| `confirm_target`    | Records what you found on the platform for an unconfirmed post: published, or not published, which fails it there.                                                                    | yes                    | yes                   | no           |
| `invite_member`     | Emails someone an invitation to the workspace, with a role. A pending invitation to the same address is replaced.                                                                     | yes                    | no                    | yes          |
| `revoke_invitation` | Revokes a pending invitation: its link stops working.                                                                                                                                 | yes                    | yes                   | no           |

Annotations are hints a client may show you; the permission checks are the server's.

### What no tool hands out [#account-data]

No tool exports your data or lists the applications that act for you. An MCP client passes whatever its tools answer to an AI model, and your export holds your sessions' addresses, your invitations and everything you wrote: the instance refuses both to every OAuth client but the [command line](/cli#account), which you sign in at your own terminal and which saves them in your own files. Account settings, in the web app, show them too.

## Limits [#limits]

- A Peal instance serves MCP clients only when its address is HTTPS, or `localhost` for development. Elsewhere, the endpoint refuses every request.
- The endpoint refuses requests sent from web pages (with an `Origin` header): connect a client that runs on your computer or on a server.
- **Account settings**, in the web app, list the clients you allowed and disconnect them; `peal account apps` lists them from the [command line](/cli#account). Leaving the workspace stops every client that acts in it.
