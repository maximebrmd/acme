Acme is a social media scheduling platform. "Acme" is a working name.

<Callout type="warning" title="Early development">
  Acme is being built in the open and has not been released yet. These pages
  describe how it is meant to work and will grow as features land.
</Callout>

## What Acme does [#what-it-does]

- **Write once.** Draft a post in one editor, then adapt it for each platform where it needs to differ.
- **Schedule across platforms.** Pick the accounts and the time. Acme publishes each post when it is due, in the time zone you scheduled it in, daylight saving included.
- **See results.** Acme collects each platform's metrics for your posts, so you can see what worked in one place.
- **Work as a team.** Organisations have members and roles, so several people can write, review and schedule together.

Acme never retries a post blindly: when a platform does not confirm whether a post went out, Acme marks it unconfirmed and asks you to check the platform before posting again.

## Self-hostable [#self-hostable]

Every outside service Acme relies on has a self-hostable default. A self-hosted instance needs only Postgres and an S3-compatible store; email, billing, captcha, AI and telemetry are optional. [Self-hosting](/self-hosting) lists the services and the settings they read.

## Fair source [#fair-source]

The source is public under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-ALv2). You may use, modify and self-host Acme for any purpose except offering it to others as a competing commercial product or service, and each release also becomes available under the Apache License 2.0 two years after it ships. That is why Acme calls itself fair source rather than open source.
